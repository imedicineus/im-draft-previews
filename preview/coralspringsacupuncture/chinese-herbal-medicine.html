Three Ideas Behind Every Formula

Balance over brute force. In a formula, each herb supports and tempers the others. Because they cooperate, no single ingredient needs a large dose. That matters, since some herbs become harmful at high amounts.

The person before the symptom. Dr. Fields looks first at your constitution: whether you tend to run warm or cool, dry or damp, robust or depleted. Helping that overall pattern is the priority, and symptoms frequently ease as it improves.

Safety before convenience. Every herb he uses is manufactured and bottled in the United States with third party lab testing on each batch.

Why Not Just Take One Herb at a High Dose?

The popular Western habit of taking one ingredient in a large amount assumes more is better. Chinese medicine takes the opposite view. A high single dose concentrates risk if your body reacts badly, and it ignores the way herbs modify one another. A formula spreads the work across several herbs at moderate amounts, each covering a different angle.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

A Tradition Refined by Observation

Chinese herbal formulas developed over thousands of years. Generations of practitioners watched how patients responded, recorded what worked, discarded what did not and passed the knowledge on. That long loop of observation and refinement resembles the scientific method stretched across centuries, and modern researchers continue to study many classic herbs.

Roles Inside a Formula

Classical formulas assign herbs to roles. A chief herb targets the main pattern. Deputies broaden or reinforce it. Assistants address side issues or soften strong effects. An envoy harmonizes the whole and guides it. When Dr. Fields modifies a formula, he usually begins with a time tested structure and adjusts a few pieces.

The Problem With Buying Herbs Online

Dr. Fields asks patients not to buy herbal products from unknown online sellers or local markets. Products without verified sourcing may contain:

  • Undeclared drugs or synthetic additives
  • Fillers that dilute the actual herbs
  • Heavy metals and other toxins
  • Mold, fungus or bacteria

Preparation Is Part of Safety

Certain herbs must be processed correctly before use, and improperly prepared herbs can be toxic. Choosing and combining them safely takes formal training. Self prescribing high doses of herbs and nutrients can, in rare cases, contribute to serious harm such as liver or kidney injury. Those risks are rare when a licensed, trained herbalist prescribes and monitors the formula.

Herbs and Medications

Some herbs interact with prescription drugs. Dr. Fields reviews your full medication and supplement list before recommending anything, and he encourages you to keep your physician informed. Never start, stop or change a prescription on your own.

Dosing and Check Ins

Dr. Fields keeps doses conservative and checks in regularly. As your body changes, he may adjust the formula, lower a dose or swap herbs. Tell him promptly about any digestive upset, rash, headache or anything that feels wrong, and stop the formula if something concerns you.

Food as the First Herb

In Chinese medicine, diet is the gentlest form of herbal care. Someone who runs cold may do better with warm, cooked meals and fewer iced drinks, a real habit change in South Florida. Someone prone to heaviness and dampness may feel better with fewer greasy and sugary foods. Simple food changes help a formula work.

A Look at Classical Diagnosis

Before choosing herbs, Dr. Fields gathers clues the way herbalists have for centuries. He asks about thirst, appetite, digestion, sweating, temperature preferences, sleep, energy through the day and, for women, the menstrual cycle. He looks at the tongue's color, shape and coating and feels the pulse at both wrists. None of these alone gives an answer, but together they form a picture of your pattern. That pattern is what the formula is written for, and it is also how he tracks progress at follow up visits.

Seasonal Care in South Florida

Our climate shapes the patterns we see. Long, humid summers can leave people feeling heavy and sluggish, with digestion that feels slow. Air conditioned offices and iced drinks can chill the body even when it is hot outside. Cold and flu season, allergy flare ups and the stress of hurricane season all show up in the clinic. Formulas can be adjusted with the seasons, and Dr. Fields may suggest a short course during a cold or a longer constitutional formula when the pattern is long standing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are your herbs organic?

Herbs are U.S. manufactured with third party testing. Ask about specific products during your visit.

Can children take herbs?

Sometimes, with appropriate formulas and doses. Dr. Fields will discuss whether it is suitable.

How quickly do herbs work?

It depends on the issue. Short term formulas for a cold act differently from long term constitutional support.

Can I take herbs while pregnant?

Some herbs are not used in pregnancy. Always tell Dr. Fields if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

Do I have to get acupuncture to receive herbs?

No. Some patients come only for herbal care, though many find the two work well together. Either way, Dr. Fields starts with a full consultation.
