1. It Targets Trigger Points

A trigger point is a tender knot within a tight band of muscle. Pressing on it may send pain to another area, like a shoulder knot causing a headache or a hip knot sending pain down the leg. Dry needling places a thin needle directly into that spot.

02

2. You May Feel a Twitch

When the needle reaches a trigger point, the muscle often twitches briefly. It can feel strange or crampy for a moment, then many people notice the muscle relaxes. Soreness for a day or two afterward is common, much like after a workout.

03

3. It Works Best With Exercise

Needling can loosen a muscle quickly, but strength and movement keep it loose. Dr. Fields gives you exercises to do at home.

04

4. Research Is Promising

Systematic reviews report that dry needling can reduce pain in the short term for conditions such as neck, shoulder and low back pain, especially when combined with exercise. Study quality varies and more long term research is needed. The idea that needling may change pain signals fits with long standing models of pain such as gate control theory.

05

5. Safety Comes First

Dry needling uses sterile, single use needles and careful technique. Tell us about blood thinners, pregnancy, implants or a strong fear of needles. Teens need parental consent, and a parent stays in the room.

Common Problems We Treat

Parents. Neck and shoulder knots from carrying kids and car seats, low back pain and tension headaches.

Desk workers. Upper back and neck tension from long hours at a computer.

What a Visit Looks Like

Dr. Fields asks how the problem started and tests your movement. He presses on muscles to find trigger points, places a few needles, rechecks your range of motion and shows you exercises. A visit typically takes 30 to 45 minutes.

Dry Needling or Acupuncture?

Both use the same thin needles. Dry needling focuses on muscle trigger points. Acupuncture uses Chinese medicine theory and may treat points far from the pain. Dr. Fields often combines them, sometimes with cupping.

Aftercare

Drink water, keep moving gently and use heat if you are sore. Save hard training for the next day.

What matters most to you. The goal you care about most keeps the plan focused.

When to Call Your Doctor Instead

Chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, high fever, severe abdominal pain or any symptom that is quickly getting worse needs medical care right away. Call 911 in an emergency.

A Note for Sports Parents

If your child plays a sport year round, recurring tightness is common. Dry needling can be part of the plan, but rest days, sleep, nutrition and varied activity matter just as much. Watch for pain that changes how your child runs or throws, and check with their doctor or athletic trainer.
