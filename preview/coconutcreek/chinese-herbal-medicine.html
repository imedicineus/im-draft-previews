1. Formulas Are Made for You

In Chinese medicine, herbs are almost always combined. A formula might contain four to fifteen ingredients, each with a role. Classical formulas have been recorded for centuries, and Dr. Fields adapts them to the individual. Two family members with colds might receive different formulas because one has chills and body aches while the other has a sore throat and fever.

02

2. Safety Comes First

Some herbs can interact with medications, including blood thinners, diabetes medications, blood pressure medications and some antidepressants. Dr. Fields reviews every medication and supplement before prescribing. Herbs are typically stopped before surgery, following your surgeon's instructions. Tell us if you are pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding, since many herbs are avoided at those times.

03

3. Quality Matters

The NCCIH notes that some Chinese herbal products sold in the United States have been contaminated or contained undeclared ingredients. We use suppliers that provide testing documentation for identity and contaminants. Please do not buy unfamiliar herbal products online without guidance.

04

4. They Come in Easy Forms

You do not need to cook pots of herbs. Most patients use concentrated granules that dissolve in warm water, or capsules and tablets. Kids may do better with small doses of granules mixed into a little juice or honey water, when age appropriate.

05

5. Herbs Work Between Visits

Acupuncture works during your appointment. Herbs keep working at home every day. That is why Dr. Fields often combines them, especially for digestion, sleep, stress, women's health and seasonal colds.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

What Families Use Herbs For

Colds and seasonal sniffles. Formulas are traditionally used at the first sign of a cold and for recovery afterward. See your doctor for high fever, breathing trouble or symptoms that linger.

Tummy troubles. Bloating, poor appetite and irregularity in kids and adults.

Sleep. Restless sleep, trouble falling asleep and waking at night.

Stress. Irritability and tension during busy seasons.

Women's health. Menstrual cramps, cycle irregularity and menopausal symptoms, coordinated with your gynecologist.

Allergies. Seasonal symptoms, used alongside your allergist's care.

Herbs for Children

Children are given lower doses and simpler formulas, and only after a careful history. Dr. Fields involves parents in every decision and asks about your pediatrician's care. Some herbs are not used in young children at all.

Food as Medicine at Home

Chinese medicine places a lot of importance on food. Warm soups, rice porridge (congee), ginger tea and cooked vegetables are traditional choices when digestion is weak or someone is recovering from illness. Dr. Fields can suggest simple recipes the whole family can eat.

What the Research Says

Research on Chinese herbal medicine is growing, and results vary by formula and condition. Reviews such as PMC7498439 discuss potential benefits and the need for more high quality trials. Dr. Fields explains what is known about any formula he suggests.

Side Effects

Most people tolerate well chosen formulas. Possible side effects include mild stomach upset or changes in bowel habits. Stop and call us if you notice a rash or feel unwell, and seek urgent care for severe symptoms.

How a Formula Is Chosen: A Family Example

Imagine two siblings, both with tummy aches. One has a poor appetite, loose stools and tires easily. The other has a bloated, tight belly that feels worse with stress and better after passing gas. In Chinese medicine, these are different patterns, so they would receive different approaches. This individualized thinking is the heart of herbal medicine, and it is why Dr. Fields asks so many questions.

Five Questions to Ask Before Taking Any Herb

What is in it? You should receive a full ingredient list.

Why this formula? Dr. Fields will explain the pattern he sees and why he chose it.

Does it interact with my medications? Every formula is screened.

How long should I take it? You will get a clear time frame and a check in date.

What should I watch for? You will know what to report and when to call.

Herbs Through the Year in South Florida

Our climate shapes which complaints show up. Summer heat and humidity are associated in Chinese medicine with heaviness, sluggish digestion and fatigue. Fall and winter bring school colds and allergy flares. Holiday travel disrupts sleep and digestion. Formulas and food advice are adjusted with the season.

Herbs and Pregnancy

Many herbs are avoided in pregnancy. Some, used with great care, may be considered for specific concerns, but only after a careful discussion with you and your obstetrician. Always tell us if you might be pregnant.

Herbs and School Season

Back to school often brings a wave of colds and sniffles to every household. In traditional practice, certain formulas are used to support resistance in people who catch colds easily, and others are used at the first signs of a cold. Good sleep, handwashing, vegetables and fruit, and staying home when sick remain the foundation. Always follow your pediatrician's advice for fevers and illness in children.

Keeping Track

Write the formula name, dose and start date on your calendar. Note any changes in the symptom you are treating, plus sleep, digestion and energy. Bring the notes to your follow up. This helps Dr. Fields decide whether to continue, adjust or stop the formula.

Storing Herbs at Home

Keep granules and capsules in a cool, dry place, sealed tightly and out of reach of children. Florida humidity can cause granules to clump, so close containers right after use.

When to Call Your Doctor Instead

Chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, high fever, severe abdominal pain or any symptom that is quickly getting worse needs medical care right away. Call 911 in an emergency.

Herbs and Allergies

Seasonal allergies are common in South Florida, where something is often blooming. In traditional practice, formulas are chosen based on whether symptoms involve sneezing and clear drainage, itchy eyes or congestion. Herbs are used alongside, not instead of, your allergist's care, and they are screened against antihistamines and other medications.

Herbs for Women Through Life Stages

From first periods to postpartum to menopause, Chinese medicine has long described patterns at each stage. Dr. Fields considers cycle history, energy, sleep and mood when choosing a formula, and coordinates with your gynecologist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are herbs safe for kids?

Some are, at proper doses and with careful selection. Always consult us and your pediatrician.

How long do I take them?

Usually a few weeks to a few months, with regular check ins.

Can I take herbs with my vitamins?

Often yes. Bring everything you take to your visit.

Do they taste bad?

Some do. Capsules avoid the taste.
