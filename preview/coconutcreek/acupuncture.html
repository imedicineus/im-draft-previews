1. The Needles Are Tiny

The first worry for most people is the needle. Acupuncture needles are solid, flexible and about as thin as a strand of hair, nothing like the hollow needles used for shots or blood draws. Most people feel a light tap or brief pinch, then a dull ache, warmth or heaviness. Many relax so much they fall asleep on the table. Every needle is sterile, used once and thrown away.

For children and needle shy adults, Dr. Fields uses fewer needles, shorter retention times and non needle options such as acupressure, gentle tapping or cupping.

02

2. Research Supports It for Several Common Problems

Acupuncture is one of the most researched complementary therapies. A large meta analysis of individual patient data (Vickers et al., 2018) pooled results from about 20,000 patients in trials for chronic pain and found acupuncture outperformed both sham and no acupuncture controls for back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, chronic headache and shoulder pain. The effects persisted over time.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health describes evidence for low back pain, neck pain, knee osteoarthritis and headache, and notes that acupuncture is generally safe in the hands of a qualified practitioner.

Scientists are still studying how it works. Research has looked at how needles interact with connective tissue (Langevin et al.) and at the nervous system's role in pain signaling, an idea that goes back to gate control theory.

03

3. The First Visit Is Mostly Talking

Expect a long conversation. Dr. Fields asks about the main problem and how it affects your day, then about sleep, digestion, energy, stress, diet and medications. He checks your pulse and looks at your tongue, two traditional Chinese medicine assessments, and examines any painful areas. Then he explains his findings and the plan before any needles go in.

Treatment usually lasts 20 to 30 minutes with needles in place while you rest. Heat lamps, gentle electrical stimulation or cupping may be added.

04

4. It Fits Into Busy Family Schedules

A typical starting plan is weekly visits for six to eight weeks, then reassessment. As things improve, visits space out. Dr. Fields also gives you things to do at home, such as stretches, acupressure points, food suggestions or a short qigong routine, so progress continues between visits.

05

5. It Works Alongside Your Family Doctor

Acupuncture is not a replacement for your pediatrician, primary care doctor or specialists. It works alongside them. Dr. Fields will ask about your medical care and, with your permission, can share his notes. New, severe or unexplained symptoms always need medical evaluation first.

What Brings Coconut Creek Families In

Parents. Neck and shoulder tension, low back pain from lifting kids, headaches, stress and sleep problems.

Expecting and new moms. Acupuncture is sometimes used during pregnancy for nausea and back pain, with adjusted point selection. Postpartum visits often focus on rest, recovery and stress. Always tell us if you are pregnant.

Teens and young athletes. Sports strains, sore shoulders and knees, and stress around exams. Parental consent is required for minors, and a parent stays in the room.

Grandparents. Knee and hip arthritis, back pain, neuropathy symptoms and sleep.

Acupuncture for Kids

Children often respond quickly and need shorter visits. Dr. Fields may use just a few needles for a few minutes, or non needle techniques. Common reasons parents ask about acupuncture for kids include tummy aches, headaches, sports injuries and trouble winding down at night. Pediatric care always involves the parent and the child's doctor.

Acupuncture and Headaches

Tension headache and migraine are among the better studied uses. Research suggests acupuncture may reduce how often headaches occur in some people. Keep a headache diary with dates, duration and possible triggers so you and Dr. Fields can see whether treatment is helping.

Acupuncture and Back Pain

Low back pain is one of the most common reasons people try acupuncture, and it is included in some clinical guidelines as an option for chronic low back pain. Treatment often combines acupuncture with heat, cupping, posture advice and gentle strengthening.

Acupuncture and Stress

Juggling work, school, activities and household needs takes a toll. Many people find acupuncture deeply relaxing. Dr. Fields often pairs it with breathing exercises and simple sleep routines.

Chinese Medicine for the Whole Family

Acupuncture is one part of Chinese medicine. Others include Chinese herbal medicine, food therapy, gua sha, cupping and qigong. Dr. Fields uses whichever tools fit the person in front of him. He also draws on functional medicine when lab work and nutrition are relevant.

Safety Notes

Side effects are usually minor: slight soreness, a small bruise or feeling tired. Tell us if you take blood thinners, have a pacemaker, a bleeding disorder or are pregnant. Acupuncture is performed only with sterile, single use needles.

Insurance

Tips for a Great First Visit

Eat a light snack an hour before. Wear loose clothing. Bring a list of medications and supplements. If you are bringing a child, plan a calm activity afterward and bring a favorite toy or book.

Bonus: Three Acupressure Points to Try at Home

Acupressure uses finger pressure on acupuncture points, with no needles. These are traditional self care points; they are not a substitute for medical care.

He Gu (LI4). In the web between the thumb and index finger. Traditionally used for headaches and tension. Avoid during pregnancy.

Nei Guan (PC6). About three finger widths above the inner wrist crease, between the two tendons. Studied for nausea, including motion sickness.

Zu San Li (ST36). About four finger widths below the kneecap, one finger width outside the shin bone. Traditionally used for digestion and energy.

Press gently with a thumb for one to two minutes while breathing slowly. Stop if anything hurts.

Family Scheduling Tips

Book the same time each week to build a routine. School hours work well for parents. For teens, avoid scheduling right before a big game or exam until you know how they respond.

Five Things to Tell Us Before Your Visit

Every medication and supplement. Including vitamins, protein powders and anything from a health food store.

Any pregnancy or chance of pregnancy. This changes which treatments and herbs are appropriate.

Allergies and sensitivities. Including to adhesives, oils or latex.

Recent procedures or injuries. Surgery, dental work, falls or new diagnoses.

What matters most to you. The goal you care about most keeps the plan focused.

When to Call Your Doctor Instead

Chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, high fever, severe abdominal pain or any symptom that is quickly getting worse needs medical care right away. Call 911 in an emergency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What age can children start acupuncture?

Children of many ages can be treated, often with non needle methods for younger kids. Ask us about your child.

Can I bring my child to my appointment?

Please call ahead so we can plan. Older kids who can sit quietly are usually fine.

How soon will I feel better?

Some people notice changes after one or two visits. Chronic problems usually take a series.

Does acupuncture hurt?

Most people feel very little. Tell Dr. Fields if anything is uncomfortable and he will adjust.

Do you treat pregnant patients?

Yes, with modified technique. Always tell us if you are pregnant.
