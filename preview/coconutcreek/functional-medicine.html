Functional Medicine in Coconut Creek: Five Things to Know

Busy parents often put their own health last. Fatigue, digestive trouble, weight that will not budge and poor sleep become the new normal. Functional medicine looks for the reasons behind those symptoms. Dr. Jonathan M. Fields, DAOM, brings training in functional medicine, blood chemistry and nutrition to our Coconut Creek clinic.

1. It Starts With Your Whole Story

The first visit is long because the details matter. Dr. Fields asks about your health history, pregnancies, illnesses, medications, diet, sleep, stress and family history. A symptom that started after a new baby, a move or a course of antibiotics may tell part of the story.

02

2. Your Labs Can Say More Than "Normal"

Bring past lab work. Dr. Fields studied blood chemistry analysis and looks at patterns across markers and over time, such as iron and ferritin, thyroid markers, blood sugar and lipids. He may suggest additional tests to discuss with your doctor. Your physician remains in charge of diagnosis.

03

3. Food Comes First

Plans center on real meals your family will actually eat. Common starting points are protein at breakfast, more vegetables and fiber, fewer sugary drinks, regular meal times and enough water. Changes are introduced a few at a time.

04

4. Supplements Only With a Reason

Many people arrive with a cabinet full of supplements. Dr. Fields reviews each one, keeps what makes sense and often simplifies. New supplements are recommended when there is a clear reason, such as a documented deficiency.

05

5. It Works With Your Doctors

Functional medicine is not a replacement for your family doctor or specialists. With your permission, Dr. Fields shares findings with them. Medication decisions stay with your prescribing physician.

Common Concerns for Coconut Creek Families

Low energy. Iron status, thyroid function, blood sugar, sleep and stress are common contributors.

Postpartum recovery. Nutrient depletion, sleep loss and thyroid changes after pregnancy are worth discussing with your doctor and our team.

Digestive symptoms. Bloating, reflux and irregularity in adults and kids.

Thyroid concerns. Including Hashimoto's, alongside endocrine care.

Blood sugar. Prediabetes, insulin resistance and family history of diabetes.

Hormonal changes. Perimenopause and menopause symptoms.

Headaches. Including links to sleep, hydration, meals and stress.

Feeding a Family Well

Healthy changes work best when the whole household eats the same food. Dr. Fields suggests simple swaps: water instead of sweetened drinks, fruit instead of packaged snacks, a vegetable at every dinner, and protein with breakfast so energy holds until lunch. Batch cooking on weekends helps busy weeknights.

Kids and Nutrition

Picky eating, constipation, tummy aches and energy swings are common questions. Dr. Fields can offer nutrition ideas that support your pediatrician's advice. Any growth, weight or development concern belongs with your pediatrician first.

Sleep for Parents

Sleep is often the first thing to go. Consistent bedtimes, a dark cool room, less screen time at night and morning light can help. Snoring, gasping or extreme daytime sleepiness should be checked by a doctor.

The Evidence

Some functional medicine steps, such as better diet, correcting deficiencies, more movement and better sleep, are strongly supported by research. Some specialty tests and supplements have less evidence. Dr. Fields explains the reasoning for each step.

Pairing With Chinese Medicine

Functional medicine often works alongside acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine, especially for stress, sleep, digestion and hormonal symptoms.

Five Signs It May Be Worth a Deeper Look

You are tired even after sleeping. Persistent fatigue deserves investigation.

Your digestion is unpredictable. Frequent bloating, reflux or irregularity is common but not normal.

Your labs are "normal" but you do not feel normal. Patterns within reference ranges may still be worth discussing.

You have a family history. Diabetes, thyroid disease or heart disease in close relatives makes prevention more important.

You have tried the basics. If diet and exercise changes have not helped, there may be other factors.

What to Bring to Your First Visit

Recent lab results from the past two to three years, a list of medications and supplements with doses, a three day food diary and your main health goals. If you are coming for a child, bring growth records and notes from your pediatrician.

Blood Sugar and Family Health

A family history of type 2 diabetes is common. Early signs, such as rising fasting glucose or A1c, can appear years before diagnosis. Diet, activity, sleep and weight all influence these markers. Changes made as a family, such as walking together after dinner and cutting back on sweetened drinks, help everyone.

Thyroid After Pregnancy

Thyroid changes are relatively common after pregnancy. Fatigue, mood changes, hair loss and weight changes can have many causes, and thyroid testing through your doctor is a reasonable step. Functional medicine can support nutrition and lifestyle factors alongside medical care.

Iron and Energy

Iron deficiency is common, especially in women with heavy periods, during and after pregnancy, and in some teens. Fatigue, shortness of breath, cold hands and feet and brittle nails are possible signs. Ferritin, iron and a complete blood count help clarify the picture. Supplementing iron without testing is not recommended.

Stress and the Body

Ongoing stress can affect sleep, digestion, blood sugar and mood. Functional medicine looks at stress as part of the plan, with practical tools such as regular meals, movement, time outside, breathing practices and qigong.

Small Steps, Steady Progress

The best plan is one you can keep. Dr. Fields usually suggests two or three changes to start, then adds more as those become habits. Follow up visits keep the plan realistic for your family's schedule.

Five Things to Tell Us Before Your Visit

Every medication and supplement. Including vitamins, protein powders and anything from a health food store.

Any pregnancy or chance of pregnancy. This changes which treatments and herbs are appropriate.

Allergies and sensitivities. Including to adhesives, oils or latex.

Recent procedures or injuries. Surgery, dental work, falls or new diagnoses.

What matters most to you. The goal you care about most keeps the plan focused.

When to Call Your Doctor Instead

Chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden weakness or numbness, high fever, severe abdominal pain or any symptom that is quickly getting worse needs medical care right away. Call 911 in an emergency.

Why Families Choose Integrative Care

Many families want care that looks at the whole person and explains things clearly. Integrative care combines traditional approaches with modern knowledge and works alongside your medical team. The goal is practical: feel better, understand why, and know what you can do at home.

Gut Health for the Whole Family

Fiber from vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains feeds helpful gut bacteria. Fermented foods such as yogurt and sauerkraut may help some people. Regular meals, enough water and daily movement support regular digestion. Persistent pain, blood in the stool or weight loss needs a doctor's evaluation.

Vitamin D in the Sunshine State

Even in Florida, vitamin D levels can be low, especially for people who work indoors or use sunscreen consistently. A simple blood test through your doctor can check your level before you supplement.
