Myth: Herbal Medicine Means Brewing Smelly Teas

Fact: Raw herb decoctions still exist, but most patients today take concentrated granules stirred into warm water or capsules. They are portable, consistent and easy to fit into a routine of tee times and travel.

03

Myth: One Herb for One Problem

Fact: Chinese medicine almost always uses formulas, combinations of several herbs organized with a clear hierarchy. A chief herb addresses the main issue, deputy herbs strengthen it, assistant herbs balance side effects and an envoy herb harmonizes the group. That logic is one of the oldest ideas in the field.

04

Myth: Everyone With the Same Symptom Gets the Same Formula

Fact: Two people with joint stiffness may get very different prescriptions. One person's stiffness worsens in cold, damp weather and improves with warmth. Another's joints feel hot and swollen. Chinese medicine treats these as different patterns, and the formulas differ accordingly.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Thousands of Years of Notes

Chinese herbal medicine is one of the oldest continuously documented medical traditions. Classic texts such as the Shang Han Lun, written around the end of the Han dynasty, describe formulas still used today, along with detailed commentary on when to use them and when not to. Generations of physicians added notes, refinements and warnings. That written record is part of what makes the tradition distinctive.

Temperature, Taste and Direction

Every herb is classified by its thermal nature (hot, warm, neutral, cool or cold), its taste (such as bitter, sweet, pungent, sour or salty) and the channels it is believed to enter. Pungent warming herbs like fresh ginger are traditionally used to disperse cold. Bitter cooling herbs are used to clear heat. Formulas are balanced so the overall effect suits the patient.

Herbs and the Aging Body

Many classical formulas were designed for the effects of aging: declining energy, joint stiffness, poor sleep, night sweats and slower digestion. In Chinese medicine terms, these are often described as deficiencies of qi, blood, yin or yang. Formulas for these patterns are among the most commonly prescribed for older active adults. Their use is based on traditional theory and clinical experience, with modern research still catching up on many of them.

Modern Science and Herbs

The most famous success story from Chinese herbal research is artemisinin, an antimalarial compound isolated from sweet wormwood. Its discoverer, Tu Youyou, shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. Researchers continue to study other herbs and formulas, though many studies are small or of uneven quality. A review in the National Library of Medicine discusses evidence and challenges in this area (PMC7498439), and the NCCIH summarizes what is known about safety and effectiveness.

Quality Control

Product quality is a real concern in the herbal market. Contamination with heavy metals, pesticides or microbes, misidentified plants and undeclared pharmaceutical ingredients have all been reported in some products. Dr. Fields uses suppliers that test for identity and contaminants. Avoid buying unlabeled or imported products of uncertain origin.

Interactions to Know

Blood thinners such as warfarin deserve special care, as do medications for diabetes, blood pressure, thyroid, heart rhythm, seizures and immune suppression. Certain herbs may increase or decrease the effects of these drugs. Disclosure and monitoring keep you safe.

Herbs Alongside Exercise

Active adults sometimes want formulas to support recovery after hard efforts or to ease achy joints during busy seasons. Dr. Fields tailors these to your training load and keeps an eye on how you respond.

Herbs and Hot Flashes

Menopausal symptoms are among the most common reasons women in Boca Raton ask about herbal medicine. Classical formulas for these patterns aim to cool and nourish. If you are using hormone therapy or have a history of hormone sensitive cancer, Dr. Fields coordinates with your physician and chooses herbs carefully.

Working With Other Treatments

Herbal formulas often accompany acupuncture and functional medicine plans, and they continue working on the days you are not in the clinic.

Myth: You Take Herbs Forever

Fact: Most formulas are used for a defined period and then tapered or changed. Some chronic conditions call for longer use with periodic review, but the goal is to need less over time, not more.

Myth: Herbs Work Instantly or Not at All

Fact: Response time depends on the problem. A formula for a fresh cold is meant to act within days. A formula for long standing fatigue or poor sleep is judged over several weeks. Dr. Fields sets expectations at the start and checks in regularly so you are never guessing.

Herbs and Joint Comfort

Classical Chinese medicine has a category of conditions called bi syndrome, often translated as painful obstruction. It describes aching, stiff or swollen joints and is divided into types according to whether symptoms worsen with cold, damp, wind or heat. Formulas for these patterns are traditionally combined with acupuncture and warmth or cooling as appropriate. For active adults with stiff knees or hands, this framework offers a different way to think about why joints feel worse on certain days. It is a traditional model rather than a modern diagnosis, and it works alongside, not in place of, orthopedic evaluation.

Food as Herbal Medicine

Many ingredients in Chinese herbal medicine are also foods: ginger, cinnamon, goji berries, Chinese yam, jujube dates, mung beans and mushrooms. Dr. Fields often suggests simple food based habits, such as warm congee for weak digestion or ginger tea on chilly mornings, that reinforce the formula in a gentle way.

Your Formula Card

Every patient receives a written record of their formula, including the herbs, dose and timing. Keep it with your medication list and share it with your physicians and pharmacist.

When to Call

Call if you notice rash, stomach upset, headache or any unexpected change after starting a formula. Stop and seek care right away for signs of an allergic reaction.

Myth: Herbs Are Unregulated

Fact: In the U.S., herbal products fall under dietary supplement rules. Oversight exists but is lighter than for drugs, which is why reputable sourcing matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly do herbs work?

For acute issues like a cold, changes may be noticed within days. For chronic patterns, expect weeks.

Will herbs affect my lab work?

Some can. Tell your physician you are taking herbs before blood tests.

Can I take herbs before surgery?

Most herbs should be stopped before surgery. Ask your surgeon and Dr. Fields for timing.

Are your herbs organic?

Supplier standards vary by product. Ask about specific formulas.

Can I get herbs without acupuncture?

Yes. Herbal consultations can be scheduled separately.
