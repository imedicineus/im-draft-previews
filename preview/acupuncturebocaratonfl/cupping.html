Myth: The Marks Are Bruises

Fact: The circular marks come from suction drawing blood into the tissue near the surface. They are usually painless and fade over several days to about a week. They are not caused by impact the way a bruise is.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Ancient and Global

Cupping has been practiced in many parts of the world, including China, the Middle East and Europe, for centuries. Historical cups were made from horn, bamboo, ceramic or glass. Today, glass fire cups and plastic pump cups are most common in clinics.

What May Be Happening

Suction lifts skin, fascia and muscle away from deeper structures. Researchers have suggested this may affect local blood flow, tissue pliability and pain signaling. Studies are limited, and the mechanisms remain under investigation.

Evidence Overview

Most cupping research has focused on neck and back pain. Some trials report short term pain improvement, but many have small samples or high risk of bias, making firm conclusions difficult. The NCCIH offers an accessible summary of the research and safety considerations.

Cupping for Recovery

Many active adults like cupping after a heavy week of play or training, when muscles feel dense and tight. Pair it with sleep, hydration and lighter training days for the best chance of feeling fresh.

Who Should Avoid Cupping

Skip it over broken skin, sunburn, rashes, varicose veins, recent injuries or areas with active inflammation. Tell us about blood thinners, easy bruising, bleeding disorders or pregnancy.

Myth: More Suction Is Better

Fact: Stronger is not always better. The right amount depends on the area, the tissue and how you respond.

Aftercare

Stay warm and covered, avoid hot tubs and saunas for the rest of the day, keep treated skin out of strong sun and drink water. Gentle movement is fine.

Combining With Other Therapies

Cupping pairs well with acupuncture, dry needling and gua sha.

Myth: Cupping Is Only for Athletes

Fact: Swimmers made it famous, but most people who receive cupping are not elite athletes. Desk workers with tight upper backs, gardeners with stiff low backs and travelers with sore shoulders after long flights are all common cupping patients.

What Dr. Fields Checks First

Before treatment, he assesses posture and movement, looks at skin condition and asks about medications and bruising history. That determines cup size, suction and placement.

How Long Does a Course Last?

Many patients try three to six weekly sessions alongside other treatment, then reassess. Some continue monthly during busy seasons of play. Dr. Fields bases recommendations on how you respond rather than a fixed package.

Frequently Asked Questions

It is best to wait until the next day.

Marks on the back are usually covered, but shoulder marks may show. Plan around events.

How often can I get cupping?

Commonly once a week, once previous marks have faded.
