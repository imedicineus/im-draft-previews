Cupping Therapy

Massage pushes into muscle. Cupping does the opposite: it lifts. That one difference is why cupping can reach tight, stuck layers in a way hands alone sometimes cannot, and why it has become one of the most requested additions to treatment in our clinics.

How Cupping Works

A cup placed on the skin creates gentle suction. That suction lifts skin, fascia and muscle up into the cup, opening space between layers and drawing fresh circulation into the area. Many people describe a pulling sensation that feels deeply relaxing once they settle into it.

Classical Chinese medicine describes cupping as moving stagnation. Modern language would talk about circulation, fascia and the nervous system. Either way, the aim is to help tight, stagnant tissue loosen.

Styles We Use

  • Stationary cupping. Cups stay in place for several minutes over tight areas.
  • Sliding cupping. With oil on the skin, I glide the cup along a muscle, like a deep reverse massage.
  • Fire cupping. A flame briefly warms the air inside a glass cup to create suction. The flame never touches you.
  • Silicone cupping. Flexible cups that are easy to move and adjust.
  • Wet cupping. A traditional method involving tiny skin punctures. We will discuss whether it is appropriate for you before considering it.

Cupping pairs well with acupuncture, dry needling and gua sha.

About the Marks

Cupping often leaves round marks that range from light pink to deep purple. They are not bruises from impact. They reflect blood drawn toward the surface from tissue where circulation has been sluggish. In Chinese medicine, darker marks are read as more stagnation. Marks usually fade within several days to about a week, and they often look lighter on later sessions.

Aftercare

  • Keep the area warm and out of strong wind or air conditioning for the day
  • Drink water
  • Skip very hot showers or saunas for a few hours
  • Gentle movement is welcome. Heavy training can wait until tomorrow.

When Cupping Is Not Right

Cupping is not used over broken skin, rashes, varicose veins or areas of active inflammation. Let me know if you are pregnant, take blood thinners or bruise easily. I adjust pressure for sensitive skin, older patients and first timers.

Cupping and Fascia

Fascia is the connective tissue wrapping every muscle. When it is restricted, muscles can feel stiff even after stretching. Because cupping lifts rather than presses, it may help separate layers that have become stuck together. That is one reason sliding cupping feels so different from a deep tissue massage: the pull works in the opposite direction.

Combining Cupping With Other Care

Cupping is rarely a stand alone treatment in our clinics. A common session might start with acupuncture to calm the nervous system, add cupping across a tight upper back and finish with stretches you can do at home.

A Note for Athletes

Training creates tight, overworked tissue. Many athletes use cupping between sessions to help them feel looser and recover. Let me know your training calendar so we can time treatment well.

First Session Tips

Eat a light meal beforehand and wear a top that is easy to remove or adjust. Tell me about sensitive areas and any events coming up where visible marks matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it hurt?

Most people find it relaxing. I start light and adjust.

How long is a session?

Cupping is usually part of a broader treatment and may take five to fifteen minutes of it.

Can I have it before an event?

If marks would be a problem, tell me and I will plan around it.

Will the marks hurt?

Usually not. The area may feel like it had a deep massage.
