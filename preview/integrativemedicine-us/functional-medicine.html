Why I Practice This Way

In my early thirties I was an athlete, active and strong, and then I was not. For about a year I was effectively disabled. I saw three neurologists, two hand surgeons, an orthopedist and an endocrinologist. I had thyroid biopsies. Each specialist looked at one piece of me, and nobody could tell me why my whole body was failing.

The turning point came from my mentor, the late Reid Eckert, a formulator for more than thirty years who had run a nutrition store for more than thirty years. He looked at the full picture, my labs, my history and my habits, and helped me build a plan around root causes. Over roughly six to eight months, I got my life back. Reid later became my partner in practice, and his approach lives on in how we work.

That experience is why I am patient with people who have been told "your labs are normal" while they still feel unwell. I have been that person.

Who Tends to Seek Us Out

Many people come to us after a long road. Common concerns include:

  • Fatigue that does not lift with rest
  • Hormone and thyroid concerns
  • Digestive trouble such as bloating, reflux or irregularity
  • Weight that will not budge despite real effort
  • Autoimmune patterns, brain fog and sleep problems
  • Fertility support

We do not diagnose or treat these as isolated labels. We look for the threads connecting them.

How the Process Works

  1. A long first conversation. We go through your story: symptoms, timeline, family history, diet, stress, sleep, medications and supplements.
  2. Testing chosen for you. Depending on your case, that may include comprehensive blood chemistry, thyroid and hormone panels, nutrient markers, digestive and stool testing, or food sensitivity testing. We only order what is likely to change the plan.
  3. A plan you can live with. Food changes, targeted supplements or herbal formulas, sleep and stress work, and often acupuncture.
  4. Monthly follow ups. We meet regularly, review how you feel and adjust.

A Conservative Approach

More supplements do not equal more health. I would rather start with fewer, well chosen steps and add only what your body shows it needs. Anything we recommend comes from tested sources, and I want your physicians in the loop. Functional medicine works alongside conventional care. It is not a substitute for it, and you should never stop a prescribed medication without your doctor.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Root Causes Versus Symptoms

Think of a smoke alarm. Pulling the battery stops the noise, but it does not put out the fire. Symptoms are often the body's alarm. Functional medicine tries to find the source: an imbalance in blood sugar, a sluggish thyroid, an inflamed gut, a nutrient shortfall, chronic stress or poor sleep. Usually several of these feed one another.

Why "Normal" Labs Can Miss Things

Standard lab ranges are built to flag disease. Many people feel unwell long before a value crosses that line. In functional medicine, we look at patterns across many markers together and how they relate to your symptoms. A value that is technically normal may still be part of the story when it sits beside other findings. This is a matter of clinical interpretation, and I explain my reasoning so you can understand your own results.

The Body as a Connected System

The thyroid affects energy, temperature and digestion. The gut influences immune function and how well you absorb nutrients. Stress hormones affect blood sugar and sleep. Sleep affects appetite and mood. When one system strains, others compensate, and eventually something gives. That is why a plan that addresses only one piece often stalls.

How Eastern Medicine Fits In

My training in Chinese medicine gives me a second lens. Classical diagnosis looks at your whole constitution, not just a lab value. Research suggests acupuncture may help with stress and sleep, and herbal formulas can be chosen to match your pattern. I find the two systems complement each other: functional testing shows what is happening chemically, and Eastern assessment shows how your body as a whole is responding. Daily practices such as medical qigong give patients something calming they can do themselves.

What Progress Can Look Like

Bodies change at their own pace. Some people notice changes in energy, sleep or digestion within weeks. Others with long standing issues need months of steady work. That is why monthly follow ups matter: we can see what is working, retest when it makes sense and adjust. By my own count I have worked with around ten thousand patients over the years, and if they taught me anything, it is that no two plans should be identical.

Food as the Foundation

Most plans start in the kitchen. We look at what you eat, when you eat and how your body responds. That might mean steadier blood sugar through regular protein, fewer ultra processed foods, more fiber and vegetables, or a short trial removing a suspected trigger. We avoid extreme diets. The goal is a way of eating you can keep.

Sleep and Stress Are Not Optional

Poor sleep and constant stress can undermine the best supplement plan. We look at your evening routine, light exposure, caffeine timing and stress load, and we use tools like breathing practice, qigong and acupuncture to help your nervous system settle.

Movement, Mindset and Community

Exercise is medicine, but the right dose depends on where you are starting. Someone exhausted may need gentle walking and qigong before anything intense. Someone who over trains may need more recovery. We also talk about support: family, friends and community make change easier. Many of our patients join a class or event as part of their plan.

Working With Your Other Doctors

Good care is a team sport. With your permission, we are glad to share findings with your physicians, and we may recommend you see a specialist when something needs further evaluation. If a symptom suggests an urgent medical issue, we will tell you so directly.

Blood Sugar and Energy

Swings in blood sugar can show up as afternoon crashes, cravings, irritability and poor sleep. Steadier meals, protein at breakfast and fewer sugary drinks are simple changes that often make a noticeable difference, and testing helps us see whether more support is needed.
