Step 2: Gather Your Information

Bring a written list of every medication, vitamin and supplement you take, with doses. Bring recent lab results or doctor's notes if you have them. Write down your main concerns and when they started. If it helps, bring a family member or friend along.

03

Step 3: The Conversation

Dr. Fields sits down with you and listens. He asks about your health history, surgeries, sleep, digestion, energy and what you would like to be able to do again, whether that is walking around the block, gardening or sleeping through the night. He reviews your medications for anything that affects treatment, such as blood thinners.

04

Step 4: A Gentle Exam

He checks how you move, where it hurts and how strong you are. He also looks at your tongue and feels the pulse at both wrists, which are traditional Chinese medicine assessments. Nothing about the exam is rushed.

05

Step 5: Getting Comfortable

The treatment table can be approached at your pace, and pillows and bolsters support your knees, neck or back. If lying flat is difficult, treatment can often be done seated or on your side. Tell us what feels best.

06

Step 6: The Treatment

Dr. Fields places very thin, sterile, single use needles at selected points. For seniors and first timers, he often starts with fewer needles and gentle technique. Most people feel a small pinch, then warmth or heaviness. You rest quietly for about 20 to 30 minutes, and someone is close by if you need anything.

07

Step 7: After the Session

Sit up slowly. Have a glass of water. Many people feel relaxed or a little sleepy, so arrange a ride the first time if you can. Dr. Fields explains the plan and how often to come back.

Acupuncture and Arthritis

Osteoarthritis is one of the most common reasons older adults seek acupuncture. Research on knee osteoarthritis in particular has been extensive. A large analysis combining individual patient data from many randomized trials found that acupuncture outperformed sham and usual care for chronic pain conditions including osteoarthritis, with benefits that largely persisted over a year (Vickers et al., 2018). Acupuncture does not rebuild cartilage, but it may help with pain and function as part of a plan that includes movement and strength.

Neuropathy

Numbness, tingling and burning in the feet are common with diabetes and can follow chemotherapy or other causes. Some studies have looked at acupuncture for peripheral neuropathy symptoms with encouraging early results, though larger trials are needed. Dr. Fields treats gently and coordinates with your physician, and he always checks the feet carefully when sensation is reduced.

Sleep in Later Life

Sleep often becomes lighter with age, with more waking during the night. Acupuncture sessions are deeply relaxing for many people, and Chinese medicine has a long tradition of treating sleep complaints. Dr. Fields also reviews daytime habits, naps, caffeine and evening routines, because small changes can make a difference.

How Acupuncture May Work

Needles stimulate nerves in skin and muscle. Those signals travel to the spinal cord and brain, where they may influence pain processing and the body's own pain relieving chemistry. Researchers have also studied effects on connective tissue (Langevin et al., 2001). In Chinese medicine, the explanation is that acupuncture restores the smooth flow of qi and blood through the body's channels. Dr. Fields draws on both views.

Safety With Medications

Acupuncture does not involve taking anything into the body, so it does not create drug interactions the way supplements can. That makes it attractive for people on several medications. However, blood thinners can increase bruising, so Dr. Fields adjusts his technique. People with pacemakers should not receive electrical stimulation. Tell us about diabetes, especially if you have reduced sensation in your feet.

Medicare and Insurance

Medicare covers acupuncture for chronic low back pain in specific circumstances and through certain qualified providers, with rules that can change. Other plans vary widely. Call our Tamarac office and we will explain your options honestly.

What the Research Says Overall

The NCCIH reviews evidence for acupuncture in several pain conditions, including low back pain, neck pain, knee osteoarthritis and headache, and notes that it is generally safe when performed by a qualified practitioner using sterile needles. Results vary from person to person.

Building a Plan

Most people start with weekly visits for several weeks. Dr. Fields checks progress at each visit using simple scales: how bad is the pain today, how did you sleep, what could you do this week that you could not before? If acupuncture is not helping after a fair trial, he will say so.

Other Options at Our Office

Your plan may include Chinese herbal medicine, functional medicine, cupping, gua sha, dry needling or gentle qigong exercises.

Acupuncture After Surgery or Illness

Recovering from a hospital stay, joint replacement or a long illness can leave you weak, achy and poorly rested. Once your surgeon or physician agrees, acupuncture can be part of a gentle recovery plan. Dr. Fields avoids surgical sites, keeps sessions short at first and builds slowly.

Caregivers Need Care Too

Many Tamarac patients are caring for a spouse or parent. Caregiving is physically and emotionally demanding, and caregivers often put their own health last. An hour of quiet treatment can be a welcome break, and Dr. Fields is glad to discuss realistic self care strategies.

A Note for Caregivers

If you help a parent or spouse get to appointments, you are welcome in the treatment room. Let us know about hearing, vision or mobility needs ahead of time so we can plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Am I too old for acupuncture?

Age alone is not a barrier. Dr. Fields adjusts treatment for your health and comfort.

I bruise easily. Can I still have it?

Usually, yes. Tell us, and Dr. Fields will use gentle technique and fewer needles.

Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed for acupuncture in Florida, though some insurance plans have their own requirements.

Will I have to undress?

Usually not. Wear loose clothing that can be rolled up. Gowns are available if needed.

Can my spouse come in with me?

Yes. Many patients bring a companion.

What if I fall asleep?

That is common and perfectly fine.
