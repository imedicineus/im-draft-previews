Gua Sha in Tamarac

Gua sha is a traditional Chinese technique that uses a smooth, rounded tool stroked along the skin to ease stiff, tight muscles. It is simple, quick and often combined with acupuncture. Dr. Jonathan M. Fields uses gentle pressure for older adults and adjusts to your comfort.

Common Uses

  • Stiff neck in the morning
  • Tight shoulders and upper back
  • Low back stiffness
  • Muscle aches
  • In traditional practice, the early stages of a cold with chills

What Gua Sha Means

The name refers to scraping (gua) and the reddish marks (sha) that rise to the surface. It has been used in Chinese households and clinics for centuries.

Research

Small studies have examined gua sha for neck and back pain and reported short term improvements in some participants. Studies have also measured increased local blood flow in treated areas. More research is needed, so gua sha is offered as a supportive therapy.

Extra Care for Older Skin

Skin becomes thinner and more fragile with age. Dr. Fields uses lighter pressure, broader tool edges and shorter treatment times for older patients, and he avoids areas with poor circulation.

Who Should Skip It

Avoid gua sha over broken skin, rashes, varicose veins, fresh injuries or areas of swelling. Tell us about blood thinners, bleeding disorders or easy bruising.

Combining Therapies

Gua sha pairs well with acupuncture and cupping.

The Tools

Clinical gua sha tools are made of smooth materials such as stainless steel, jade, horn or ceramic. A traditional soup spoon was once a common household tool. Dr. Fields cleans and disinfects tools between patients.

Self Care Between Visits

Dr. Fields may show you or a family member a very gentle version of gua sha to use at home on the neck or shoulders, using light pressure and a smooth tool. He will also suggest stretches that help prevent stiffness from returning.

Step by Step: Easing a Stiff Neck at Home

Warm it first. A warm towel for a few minutes softens tight muscles.

Move gently. Slowly turn your head side to side, then tip your ear toward each shoulder. Stay in a comfortable range.

Roll the shoulders. Lift, roll back and drop the shoulders five times.

Check your setup. Raise screens and reading material to eye level to reduce strain.

Call if it lingers. Neck pain with numbness, weakness, dizziness or headache needs medical evaluation.

Questions to Ask Before Any Bodywork

What will you be doing? A clear explanation helps you relax.

Will it leave marks? Knowing what to expect avoids worry later.

What should I tell you? Share medications, skin sensitivities, recent falls and any areas that hurt to touch.

Can I stop at any time? Always yes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will gua sha leave marks?

Often yes. They usually fade in two to four days.

Is it safe with arthritis?

It is not done directly over inflamed joints but can help nearby tight muscles.

Can I have it with my blood thinner?

Sometimes, with very light pressure. Tell us first.
