Dry Needling in Tamarac

Some muscle pain does not respond to stretching, heat or massage. A knot in the shoulder blade, a tight spot in the buttock that sends pain down the leg, a calf that cramps at night. Dry needling targets these trigger points directly, and Dr. Jonathan M. Fields, who is certified in dry needling, adapts the technique for older adults.

Step 2: Deciding If It Fits

Dry needling is not right for everyone. Dr. Fields considers your medications, especially blood thinners, your skin condition, any implants and your comfort with needles. If another approach suits you better, he will say so.

03

Step 3: Gentle Needling

A thin, sterile needle is placed into the trigger point. You may feel a quick twitch or a deep ache. For seniors, Dr. Fields often uses fewer points and a lighter approach. Needles are removed promptly.

04

Step 4: Move and Recheck

You will be asked to move the area. Many people notice the muscle feels looser. Dr. Fields then shows you a stretch or simple exercise to do at home.

05

Step 5: Aftercare

Mild soreness for a day or two is normal. Warmth, gentle movement and water help. Call if anything feels unusual.

What Trigger Points Are

Trigger points are tender spots in a tight band of muscle that can refer pain to other areas. They may develop from overuse, poor posture, injury, long periods of sitting or stress. In older adults, muscles that have stiffened over years of habit are common sources.

How Needling May Help

Needling a trigger point may reduce local muscle tension and change pain signaling in the nervous system. The idea that sensory input can influence pain perception goes back to the gate control theory (Melzack and Wall, 1965). Research on dry needling shows promising short term pain relief for some conditions, though study quality varies and it works best combined with exercise.

Dry Needling Versus Acupuncture

Both use the same type of needle. Dry needling focuses on muscle trigger points found by touch. Acupuncture follows Chinese medicine theory and treats both local and whole body patterns. Dr. Fields is trained in both and often blends them.

Safety for Seniors

Thin skin, reduced muscle mass, blood thinners and diabetes all call for extra care. Dr. Fields uses shallow technique where appropriate, avoids areas of poor circulation and checks skin carefully. He does not needle over joint replacements or recent surgical sites.

Exercises That Help Results Last

Dry needling can loosen a tight muscle, but strength and movement help keep it that way. Dr. Fields often suggests simple exercises such as shoulder blade squeezes, chin tucks, gentle hip bridges and calf stretches against a wall. Each is chosen to fit your ability and can be done at home without equipment.

When to See Your Doctor First

New pain after a fall, pain with fever, numbness or weakness that is getting worse, or pain with bladder or bowel changes needs medical evaluation before needling.
