Nutrition Comes First

A plan that ignores food rarely works for long. Dr. Fields looks at what you eat in a typical day, how much protein you get, your fiber intake, how much water you drink and whether appetite or chewing problems limit your choices. Older adults often need more protein than they realize to maintain muscle, and many do not drink enough water, especially in Florida's heat.

Medications and Nutrients

Some common medications can affect nutrient levels over time. Long term acid blockers may lower absorption of B12, iron and magnesium. Metformin has been associated with lower B12. Diuretics can affect potassium and magnesium. Dr. Fields reviews your medications with this in mind and shares any concerns with your prescriber. He never changes or stops a prescription himself.

Blood Sugar

Fasting glucose, hemoglobin A1c and sometimes fasting insulin give a picture of blood sugar control. Food choices, meal timing, muscle strength, sleep and stress all influence these numbers. Functional medicine plans often focus here because blood sugar affects energy, heart health, nerves and memory.

Thyroid

A full thyroid picture may include TSH, free T4, free T3 and thyroid antibodies. Fatigue, feeling cold, constipation, dry skin and thinning hair can relate to thyroid function, though they have other causes too. Dr. Fields reviews these markers with you and coordinates with your endocrinologist or primary care doctor.

Digestion and the Gut

Digestive complaints are common in later life. Low stomach acid, slower movement through the gut, changes in gut bacteria and medication side effects can all play a role. Plans may include changes to fiber, fluids, meal size and food choices, with targeted support when appropriate.

Anemia

Anemia can cause fatigue, shortness of breath and weakness. Iron deficiency, B12 or folate deficiency and chronic disease are common causes. Recurring anemia should be investigated with your physician, and functional medicine can look at diet and absorption alongside that work.

Inflammation and Joints

Joint aches and stiffness are common with age. Food choices, body weight, blood sugar, sleep and activity level can all influence inflammation. Dr. Fields may suggest dietary changes and pairs functional medicine with acupuncture for pain when helpful.

Supplements, Sensibly

Many Tamarac patients arrive with a bag of supplements. Dr. Fields reviews each one: is it needed, is the dose sensible, does it duplicate something else and could it interact with a medication? Often the result is fewer products, not more.

What the Evidence Supports

Functional medicine combines many approaches. Some, such as improving diet quality, correcting documented deficiencies, staying active and sleeping well, have broad scientific support. Others, such as certain specialty tests and supplements, have less. Dr. Fields explains the evidence for each recommendation so you can decide.

Step by Step: Preparing for Your First Visit

Gather records. Collect lab reports from the past two to three years, a list of diagnoses and a list of surgeries with approximate dates.

List your medications. Include doses and how long you have taken each one.

Keep a three day food diary. Write down what you eat and drink, and when, for three ordinary days. Do not change your habits for the diary.

Note your symptoms. Which bother you most? When did they start? What makes them better or worse?

Write your goals. Perhaps you want more energy for your grandchildren, better sleep, fewer stomach problems or steadier blood sugar.

Sleep and Recovery

Sleep changes with age. Many older adults wake more often at night. Pain, frequent urination, reflux, sleep apnea and medications can all interfere. Functional medicine looks for these contributing factors and often pairs practical changes, such as an earlier dinner, a consistent bedtime and morning light, with other therapies. Suspected sleep apnea should always be evaluated by a physician.

Muscle and Strength

Muscle mass naturally declines with age, and loss of strength is linked to falls and loss of independence. Adequate protein and regular strength and balance exercise help. Dr. Fields may suggest simple home routines and qigong for balance, alongside dietary changes.

Heart Health

Cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure and blood sugar are all part of heart health. Functional medicine looks at how diet, weight, activity, sleep and stress influence these numbers. Decisions about cholesterol or blood pressure medication remain with your physician.

Memory and Mood

Many people worry about memory as they age. Low B12, thyroid changes, poor sleep, dehydration, depression and some medications can affect thinking and mood. These are worth checking with your physician. Any sudden or significant change in memory needs prompt medical evaluation.

Working With Your Care Team

Many Tamarac patients see a primary care doctor plus several specialists. Functional medicine works best when everyone is informed. With your permission, Dr. Fields can share his findings and suggestions with your physicians, and he will ask you to bring any changes in your prescriptions to the next visit. You stay in charge of every decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will this replace my doctor?

No. Functional medicine works alongside your primary care physician and specialists.

Do I have to change my whole diet?

No. Changes are introduced gradually and fit your preferences and budget.

Will I have to take a lot of supplements?

Usually not. Many people take fewer after a review.

Is lab work covered by Medicare?

Some tests may be covered when ordered for appropriate reasons by your physician. Call us to discuss.

Can a family member come to visits?

Yes. We encourage it, especially for the findings visit.
