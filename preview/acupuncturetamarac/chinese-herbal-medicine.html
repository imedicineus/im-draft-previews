Nutrition Roots

Our Tamarac office grew out of a nutrition practice. Dr. Fields learned from his late mentor and partner, Reid Eckert, how much food and targeted nutrition matter. Chinese herbal medicine shares that view. Many herbs are also foods, and Dr. Fields often recommends simple dietary habits alongside a formula.

How Formulas Are Built

A formula is a team of herbs with assigned roles. The chief herb addresses the main problem. Supporting herbs strengthen or broaden its effect. Others protect digestion or balance any harsh qualities. This careful structure is one reason formulas can be gentler than single, concentrated herbs.

Classic Formulas for Later Life

Chinese medicine has long described the changes of aging in terms of declining qi, blood, yin and yang. Formulas such as Liu Wei Di Huang Wan, a traditional yin nourishing formula, and Bu Zhong Yi Qi Tang, traditionally used for fatigue and weak digestion, have been used for centuries in older patients. These traditional uses are not the same as established medical effects, and Dr. Fields will explain what is known and unknown about any formula he recommends.

Interactions Dr. Fields Watches For

Blood thinners. Certain herbs may affect clotting, so they are avoided or used with great caution.

Diabetes medications. Some herbs may influence blood sugar, so monitoring is important.

Blood pressure and heart medications. Several herbs can affect heart rate or blood pressure.

Thyroid medication. Timing can matter to avoid absorption issues.

Transplant and immune suppressing drugs. Herbs are generally avoided or used only with physician input.

Always tell your pharmacist and physician that you are taking an herbal formula.

Quality Matters

The herbal products market varies in quality. Reports have found contamination or mislabeling in some products sold online or imported without oversight. Dr. Fields uses suppliers that test for identity, heavy metals and microbial contamination. Avoid unlabeled herbs and products with exaggerated health claims.

What Research Shows

Scientific interest in Chinese herbs is substantial. Artemisinin, derived from sweet wormwood, became an important malaria treatment and earned its discoverer a share of the 2015 Nobel Prize. A review in the National Library of Medicine discusses the evidence and methodological challenges in herbal research (PMC7498439). The NCCIH notes that some Chinese herbal products have been contaminated and that rigorous research is limited for many uses. Dr. Fields takes these cautions seriously.

Herbs and Digestion in Older Adults

Digestive strength often declines with age, showing up as poor appetite, bloating, loose stools or constipation. In Chinese medicine, digestion is considered the foundation of energy, so many plans begin there. Warm, cooked foods, regular mealtimes and gentle digestive formulas are common recommendations.

Herbs Combined With Acupuncture

Herbs work every day, while acupuncture visits happen weekly or less. Using both can help maintain momentum between sessions. See our acupuncture and functional medicine pages for more.

Before a Procedure

Many herbs should be stopped a week or more before surgery, dental work or certain tests. Let Dr. Fields and your surgeon know about any upcoming procedure.

Step by Step: Starting an Herbal Formula

First, bring everything you take. Put all prescriptions, over the counter products, vitamins and supplements in a bag and bring it to your visit. This is the single most important safety step.

Second, the review. Dr. Fields checks each item for possible interactions. Some herbs can affect blood clotting, blood sugar or blood pressure, so these are given particular attention, especially for people taking warfarin, other blood thinners, diabetes medications or blood pressure medications.

Third, a starting dose. For older adults, formulas often start at a lower dose. Granules, capsules or tablets are chosen based on what you find easiest to swallow and remember.

Fourth, watch and report. During the first weeks, note any changes in digestion, sleep, energy or how you feel. Call the office with questions at any time.

Fifth, the follow up. At your next visit, the formula may be adjusted, continued or stopped. Formulas are rarely meant to be taken forever.

Herbs and Food in Everyday Life

Chinese medicine has long treated food as the first medicine. Warm, cooked meals are often recommended for people with weak digestion. Ginger, scallion, congee (rice porridge) and simple soups appear in traditional dietary advice. Dr. Fields brings his nutrition background to these conversations, combining traditional ideas with modern guidance on protein, fiber and hydration.

Quality and Sourcing

Herbal products vary in quality. Dr. Fields uses suppliers that provide testing information for identity and contaminants. We do not recommend buying unfamiliar herbal products online, since contamination and mislabeling have been documented in the supplement market.

Communicating With Your Doctors

With your permission, Dr. Fields can send a summary of your herbal formula to your physician. Keeping everyone informed is part of safe care, especially for people who see several specialists.

Before Surgery or Procedures

Many herbs and supplements should be stopped before surgery or dental procedures. Tell your surgeon what you take and ask when to stop. As a general practice, many surgeons ask patients to stop herbal products well ahead of a scheduled operation.

Making Your Visit Comfortable

If you need extra time getting on and off the treatment table, or would like help with positioning, just let us know when you book. Bring a list of questions so nothing is forgotten.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take herbs if I am on many medications?

Sometimes. It depends on the specific medications. Dr. Fields reviews your list first and will tell you honestly if herbs are not a good idea.

How big are the capsules?

Most are standard capsule size. If swallowing is difficult, granules or liquid forms may work better.

How long until I notice a difference?

Gentle formulas for chronic concerns are usually judged over several weeks.

Do I need to tell my doctor?

Yes, always.

What if I have a reaction?

Stop the formula and call us. Seek urgent care for any signs of an allergic reaction such as swelling or difficulty breathing.
