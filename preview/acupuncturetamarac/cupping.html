Cupping Therapy in Tamarac

Cupping looks unusual, but it is one of the most relaxing treatments we offer. Rounded cups use gentle suction to lift the skin and muscles beneath, which many people describe as a deep, comforting stretch. At our Tamarac office, Dr. Jonathan M. Fields keeps suction light for older adults and anyone new to the technique.

Step 2: Placing the Cups

Cups are placed on the back, shoulders or other muscular areas. Suction is created with a small hand pump or a brief flame inside a glass cup. The flame never touches your skin.

03

Step 3: Rest

Cups usually stay in place for five to ten minutes. Some people feel tightness at first that eases quickly.

04

Step 4: Removal

Cups are released by letting air in, with no pulling. Round pink to purple marks may appear and usually fade within a week or so.

05

Step 5: Going Home

Stay warm, drink water and avoid hot baths or strong sun on the area for the rest of the day.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

What Cupping Is Used For

  • Stiff upper back and shoulders
  • Low back tension
  • Muscle aches
  • Feeling tense or stressed
  • In traditional practice, chest congestion with a cold

History

Cupping has been part of traditional healing in China, the Middle East and Europe for centuries. Early cups were made of animal horn or bamboo.

The Marks

Marks come from small capillaries releasing blood near the surface under suction. They are usually painless. In older adults with fragile skin, Dr. Fields uses lighter suction and shorter times to keep marks mild.

What Research Says

Studies on cupping mostly focus on neck and back pain. Some suggest short term pain relief, but many studies are small and further research is needed. The NCCIH provides a summary of research and safety.

Who Should Avoid It

Cupping is not done over broken skin, varicose veins, recent injuries, areas with poor circulation or swollen skin. Tell us if you take blood thinners, bruise easily or have a bleeding disorder.

Pairing With Other Care

Cupping is often combined with acupuncture and gua sha.

Types of Cupping

Stationary cupping. Cups are placed and left in one spot. This is the most common method.

Moving cupping. With oil on the skin, a cup is glided slowly along the muscle. It feels like a deep massage. Dr. Fields uses light suction for this technique with older adults.

Flash cupping. Cups are applied and removed quickly several times. It is gentle and leaves fewer marks.

Comfort and Positioning

Lying face down can be hard for people with neck problems, breathing conditions or reflux. Cupping can be done lying on your side or sitting supported in a chair. Pillows and bolsters help you stay comfortable.

Step by Step at Home Afterward

Keep covered. Wear a shirt over the treated area and avoid cold drafts or direct air conditioning on your back.

Drink water. Have a glass of water when you get home.

Rest. Avoid strenuous exercise for the rest of the day.

Watch the marks. Note how long they take to fade and mention it at your next visit.

Making Your Visit Comfortable

If you need extra time getting on and off the treatment table, or would like help with positioning, just let us know when you book. Bring a list of questions so nothing is forgotten.

Questions to Ask Before Any Bodywork

What will you be doing? A clear explanation helps you relax.

Will it leave marks? Knowing what to expect avoids worry later.

What should I tell you? Share medications, skin sensitivities, recent falls and any areas that hurt to touch.

Can I stop at any time? Always yes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cupping safe if I bruise easily?

Often yes, with very light suction. Tell us first.

Can I have cupping lying on my side?

Yes. Positioning is adjusted for comfort.

How often?

Commonly once a week during a course of treatment.
