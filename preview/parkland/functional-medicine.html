Functional Medicine for Parkland Patients

Functional medicine, as I practice it, means looking for patterns across digestion, sleep, stress, movement and labs instead of chasing only one number. Parkland patients who feel "not quite right" despite normal reports often want this kind of review.

01

The Short Version

I start by listening to your full story. Then we look at labs as patterns, put food and daily habits first and use supplements only when there is a clear reason. The plan is meant to work with your other doctors, not against them.

02

Where It Tends to Help

Common concerns include low energy, blood sugar swings, digestive discomfort, thyroid related questions, menstrual and midlife changes, and recovery after long stress. This is not emergency care and not a replacement for specialty medicine when that is needed.

03

What to Expect

Visits are conversation heavy. I ask about meals, sleep, mood, movement, medications and prior workups. If labs are useful, I explain why before ordering. Follow-ups focus on what changed and what to try next, in small steps you can keep.

04

Food First

I would rather improve breakfast, protein, fiber and meal timing than start with a long supplement list. Parkland family schedules are busy, so plans have to fit real kitchens and real calendars.

05

Labs as Patterns

A value inside a reference range can still be worth discussing in context. I look at trends, symptoms and how markers sit together. When something needs a medical specialist, I say so.

Supplements With a Reason

If we use a supplement, you will know the target, the dose and when we will reassess. More bottles are not the goal.

Working With Your Doctors

Bring recent labs and specialist notes when you have them. With permission I can share my recommendations so your care team stays aligned.

Energy, Iron and Thyroid Questions

Fatigue has many contributors. We sort through sleep, iron status, thyroid context, blood sugar habits and stress load before guessing. Testing is chosen, not automatic.

Steady Blood Sugar Habits

Balanced meals, walking after eating and consistent sleep often help more than extreme diets. I keep advice practical for Parkland households.

A Realistic Pace

I prefer a few high yield changes over a dozen fragile ones. Parkland calendars fill fast, so the plan has to survive soccer nights and early school mornings.

When Specialty Care Comes First

Chest pain, sudden neuro changes, unexplained weight loss or other red flags belong in urgent or specialty medical channels first. Functional style review waits until you are safe.

Records Worth Bringing

Prior thyroid panels, iron studies, recent physicals and specialist letters save time. Photos of supplement labels help too.

Digestion as a Clue

Bloating, irregularity and food fear all shape energy and mood. We map meals before adding specialty tests so the first changes are grounded in what you already eat.

Stress Load Without Drama

I ask about caregiving, work hours and recovery time without turning the visit into a lecture. Small boundaries and sleep protection often matter as much as any capsule.

Follow-Up That Respects Your Week

Check-ins are scheduled around what you can keep. If a plan only works on perfect weeks, it is the wrong plan for Parkland life.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will functional medicine style care fit my situation in Parkland?

After history and exam I will say whether functional medicine style care is a reasonable next step or whether another path makes more sense first.

How soon might I notice changes?

Some people feel easier motion within a few visits. Stubborn patterns often need a longer arc. I reassess on a set schedule rather than guessing.

Is the first visit different?

Yes. Visit one emphasizes listening, exam and a clear plan. Treatment may begin that day when appropriate, always with your okay.

What should I wear?

Loose, comfortable clothing that lets us reach the area we need. Athletic layers work well for most Parkland patients.
