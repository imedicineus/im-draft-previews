Functional Medicine: A Letter to Parkland Families

Dear neighbor,

Many people come to me after being told their tests are normal, yet they still feel tired, foggy, bloated or not themselves. I understand how frustrating that is. Functional medicine is my way of taking a closer look. I want to explain what I do, how I do it and how it fits with the care you already receive.

I Start by Listening

Our first visit is long on purpose. I ask about your health from childhood to now, illnesses, medications, pregnancies, diet, sleep, stress, work, relationships and environment. I look for the timeline: when did you last feel well, and what changed? Often the story points to the most useful next step.

03

I Read Labs as Patterns

I trained in blood chemistry analysis and functional endocrinology. When I look at your labs, I do not stop at whether each number is in or out of range. I look at how markers relate to each other and how they have changed over the years. Iron and ferritin, thyroid markers, glucose and A1c, lipids and inflammatory markers often tell a clearer story together. I may suggest additional testing for you to discuss with your physician, who remains responsible for medical diagnosis.

04

I Put Food First

Most plans I write begin with food, sleep and movement, because those are the foundations that have the strongest evidence. I build plans around meals you actually like and can prepare. I would rather you make three changes that stick than twenty you abandon in a week.

05

I Use Supplements Carefully

Many people arrive with a bag full of supplements. I review each one. Often I recommend fewer. When I suggest something new, it is for a specific reason, such as a documented deficiency, and I consider your medications.

06

I Work With Your Doctors

Functional medicine is not a replacement for your primary care doctor or specialists. With your permission, I share my findings with them. Medication decisions stay with the physicians who prescribe them.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Concerns I Commonly Help With

Fatigue. Iron status, thyroid, blood sugar, sleep and stress are frequent contributors.

Digestive symptoms. Bloating, reflux, constipation and loose stools.

Thyroid concerns. Including Hashimoto's, in partnership with your endocrinologist.

Blood sugar and weight. Insulin resistance, prediabetes and a family history of diabetes.

Hormonal transitions. Perimenopause and menopause, alongside your gynecologist's care.

Headaches, sleep and mood. Often connected to meals, hydration, sleep and stress.

What I Have Learned About Energy

Fatigue is rarely about one thing. In my experience, it is often a combination: a little low iron, poor sleep, skipped breakfasts, a stressful season and not enough movement. Addressing several of these together tends to help more than chasing a single cause.

What I Have Learned About Digestion

Digestion is affected by what you eat, how fast you eat, stress, medications and sleep. I often start with simple steps: regular meals, chewing well, more fiber from vegetables and beans, enough water and a short walk after dinner. When needed, I look further.

The Honest Truth About Evidence

Some parts of functional medicine rest on strong research, such as improving diet quality, correcting deficiencies, exercising and sleeping well. Other parts, such as some specialty tests and supplements, have weaker support. I will tell you which is which.

Functional Medicine and Chinese Medicine Together

My training lets me combine lab based thinking with Chinese medicine. For stress, sleep, digestion and hormonal symptoms, I often pair functional medicine with acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine.

Questions I Encourage You to Ask Me

What do you think is going on? I will explain my assessment in both Chinese medicine and everyday terms.

What should I expect, and when? I will give you realistic goals and a time frame for reassessing.

What can I do at home? There is almost always something.

When should I see my doctor instead? I will tell you if something needs medical evaluation first.

When to Seek Medical Care First

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, a severe headache unlike any before, high fever or rapidly worsening symptoms need prompt medical care. Call 911 in an emergency.

What to Bring to Our First Visit

Lab results from the past two or three years, a list of your medications and supplements with doses, a short food diary from three ordinary days and a list of your top concerns. If you have a medical summary from your physician, bring that too.

Labs I Often Review

Depending on your history, I may look at a complete blood count, metabolic panel, lipid panel, A1c and fasting insulin, a thyroid panel with antibodies, ferritin and iron studies, vitamin D, B12 and folate, and inflammatory markers. Not everyone needs every test. I prioritize the ones most likely to change what we do.

Sleep Is Medicine

I ask about sleep in almost every visit. Consistent bed and wake times, morning light, a cool dark bedroom and fewer screens at night help many people. If you snore loudly, gasp at night or feel very sleepy during the day, please see your doctor about sleep apnea.

Movement That Fits Your Life

You do not need a gym. Walking after meals, a few strength exercises at home and a short qigong routine can make a real difference in energy and blood sugar. I suggest a starting point that matches where you are today.

Small Changes, Followed Over Time

At each follow up, we review what changed in how you feel and, when it makes sense, in your labs. Then we adjust. I would rather move steadily than overwhelm you.

Functional Medicine for Teens and Families

Teenagers juggling school, sports and activities often skip meals and short their sleep. Simple steps, such as a real breakfast, a water bottle and a consistent bedtime, can help energy and focus. I work with parents and the teen's pediatrician.

Serving Parkland and Its Neighbors

Our Coral Springs and Tamarac clinics are convenient for families in Parkland, Heron Bay, Coral Springs, Coconut Creek and the surrounding area. My office can help you find appointment times that fit around school and work.

What I Hope You Take Away

Good care is a partnership. I will listen carefully, explain what I find, tell you honestly what the evidence says and give you practical steps you can take at home. You bring your questions, your goals and your feedback. Together, we adjust until the plan fits.

Iron, Thyroid and Women's Energy

Many women I see have some combination of heavy periods, low ferritin, thyroid changes and poor sleep. Each one alone can sap energy; together they can be exhausting. Testing through your doctor clarifies the picture, and a plan that addresses each piece often helps.

Blood Sugar Without Extremes

You do not need an extreme diet to improve blood sugar. Protein and fiber at each meal, fewer sweetened drinks, a walk after dinner and better sleep are a solid foundation.
