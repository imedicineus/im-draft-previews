What I Am Looking For

A trigger point is a tender knot within a tight band of muscle. Pressing on it often reproduces your pain, sometimes in a different spot. A knot in the upper shoulder can cause headaches; one in the hip can send pain down the leg. I find these by examining how you move and by careful touch.

03

What I Do

I place a thin, sterile needle into the trigger point. The muscle often twitches briefly, which can feel strange or crampy for a moment. Then it usually relaxes. I recheck your range of motion right away and teach you an exercise or two to keep the gains.

04

What the Research Says

Systematic reviews suggest dry needling can reduce pain in the short term for conditions such as neck, shoulder and low back pain, particularly when combined with exercise. Study quality varies and long term data are limited. Proposed mechanisms include local changes in muscle tension and changes in pain signaling, consistent with frameworks like gate control theory.

Who I Commonly Treat

Student athletes. Soccer, lacrosse, baseball, swimming and dance place repeated demands on shoulders, hips and legs. I treat teens with parental consent and a parent in the room.

Adult athletes. Runners, cyclists, golfers, tennis and pickleball players with tight calves, hips, shoulders and forearms.

Desk workers. Neck and upper back tension from long hours at a screen.

Parents. Low back and shoulder strain from lifting and carrying.

How It Differs From Acupuncture

The needles are the same. Dry needling targets muscle trigger points based on anatomy. Acupuncture follows Chinese medicine theory and may use points far from the pain. I often combine them, along with cupping.

What to Expect Afterward

Soreness similar to a hard workout for a day or two is common. Drink water, keep moving gently and use heat. Save intense training for the next day.

Safety

I use sterile, single use needles and careful technique, especially near the chest wall. Please tell me about blood thinners, pregnancy, implants or a strong fear of needles.

Keeping the Results

Dry needling opens a window. Exercise, sleep, recovery days and good training habits keep it open. I will give you a simple home plan, and I am happy to coordinate with your coach, athletic trainer or physical therapist.

Questions I Encourage You to Ask Me

What do you think is going on? I will explain my assessment in both Chinese medicine and everyday terms.

What should I expect, and when? I will give you realistic goals and a time frame for reassessing.

What can I do at home? There is almost always something.

When should I see my doctor instead? I will tell you if something needs medical evaluation first.

When to Seek Medical Care First

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, a severe headache unlike any before, high fever or rapidly worsening symptoms need prompt medical care. Call 911 in an emergency.
