Gua Sha for Parkland Patients

Gua sha is a smooth-edged tool technique I use to stroke lubricated skin over tight muscle and fascia. Parkland patients often ask for it when the neck, upper back or jaw feel locked.

The Short Version

After oil or lotion, I stroke a smooth tool along the tissue with steady pressure. Temporary redness or petechiae can appear and usually fade in a few days. The goal is easier motion and less guarding in the treated area.

02

Where It Tends to Help

Common uses include neck and shoulder tension, upper back tightness, and some headache patterns linked to muscle guarding. Facial gua sha uses lighter pressure and different goals than body work.

03

What to Expect

I explain pressure before we start and adjust as we go. You should feel deep pressure, not sharp pain. Afterward the area may feel warm. Gentle movement and water help.

04

About the Marks

Redness is a normal response for many people and is not an injury when technique is appropriate. Cover treated skin for sun if it is still pink. Call if marks seem excessive or skin breaks.

05

Safety

I avoid gua sha over broken skin, infection, sunburn, and in people with certain bleeding risks. Tell me about blood thinners and skin conditions before we begin.

Body Gua Sha and Facial Gua Sha

Body work uses firmer strokes over larger muscles. Facial work is lighter and more about circulation and soft tissue ease. I will say which approach we are using.

Research Snapshot

Clinical research on gua sha is still early. Some studies report short term reductions in neck pain and stiffness. I treat those findings as supportive, not definitive.

When Gua Sha Fits the Visit

If muscle guarding is obvious and skin is healthy, gua sha can be the main manual method that day. If tissue is too irritable, we choose a gentler option.

Home Care After Strokes

Keep the area comfortable, drink water and avoid harsh scrubbing. Mild warmth is usually fine once redness settles.

Pressure Check-Ins

I ask for feedback during strokes so pressure stays productive. Silence is not required; coaching helps.

Yes. Visit one emphasizes listening, exam and a clear plan. Treatment may begin that day when appropriate, always with your okay.
