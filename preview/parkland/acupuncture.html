What Acupuncture Is

Acupuncture is the placement of very fine, sterile needles at specific points on the body. It is one part of Chinese medicine, a system of care that has developed over more than two thousand years. I diagnose using traditional methods, such as feeling the pulse and looking at the tongue, alongside a modern orthopedic exam and your medical history. Then I choose points that address both your main complaint and the broader pattern behind it.

03

What the Research Tells Us

I think you deserve to know what the evidence says. One of the most important studies in the field, a large individual patient data meta analysis led by Andrew Vickers in 2018, pooled data from about 20,000 patients in randomized trials. It found that acupuncture worked better than sham acupuncture and better than no acupuncture for chronic back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, chronic headache and shoulder pain, and that benefits largely persisted a year later.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health also summarizes evidence for several pain conditions and notes acupuncture is generally safe when done by a qualified practitioner.

I find the research on mechanisms fascinating. Dr. Helene Langevin's work showed that turning an acupuncture needle engages the connective tissue around it (Langevin et al.). Other researchers described fluid filled spaces within connective tissue that may be relevant (Benias et al.). And the classic gate control theory of pain offers a framework for how sensory input can change how pain is felt. None of this is the final word, but it is an exciting time to practice.

What acupuncture is not: it is not an answer for everything, and it does not replace your physician. I will always tell you if I think you need medical evaluation first.

04

What Your First Visit Will Feel Like

I will spend a good part of our first visit listening. I want to know about the problem that brought you in, and also about your sleep, digestion, energy, stress and history. Then I will examine you, explain what I find and describe my plan before we begin.

The needles are about as thin as a hair. Most people feel a brief pinch or nothing at all, followed by a dull ache, warmth or heaviness near the point. Then you rest, usually for 20 to 30 minutes. Many of my patients fall asleep. I may add heat, gentle electrical stimulation, cupping or gua sha.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

What I Commonly Treat

Back and neck pain. These are among the best studied uses of acupuncture.

Headaches and migraines. Research suggests acupuncture may reduce headache frequency for some people.

Joint pain. Including knee osteoarthritis and shoulder pain.

Stress, anxiety and sleep. Many patients tell me they feel calmer and sleep better during a course of treatment.

Digestive complaints. Nausea, bloating and irregularity, often with dietary advice and herbs.

Women's health. Menstrual pain and menopausal symptoms, alongside your gynecologist's care.

Sports injuries. Strains, tight muscles and overuse problems, often combined with dry needling.

How Many Visits You May Need

I usually suggest a starting plan of weekly visits for six to eight weeks, then we reassess together. Some problems respond in a few visits. Long standing ones usually take longer. I will not ask you to commit to an open ended plan. We will set goals and check them.

What I Ask of You

Tell me what is changing and what is not. Try the simple home suggestions I give you, which may include stretches, acupressure, food changes or a short qigong routine. Keep your other doctors informed, and I will do the same with your permission.

About Safety

I use only sterile, single use needles. Side effects are usually mild: a bit of soreness, a small bruise or feeling sleepy afterward. Please tell me if you take blood thinners, have a pacemaker, have a bleeding disorder or are pregnant, so I can adjust my approach.

Acupuncture for Your Teens

Parkland is full of active, hardworking teenagers juggling school, sports and activities. I treat teens with parental consent and with a parent in the room. Many need only a few needles. Common reasons include sports injuries, headaches and stress.

Beyond the Needles

Acupuncture is one tool. I also use Chinese herbal medicine, functional medicine, nutrition and qigong. I choose the tools that fit you.

Insurance

Some insurance plans include acupuncture benefits. Medicare covers acupuncture for chronic low back pain under specific circumstances and with qualified providers. My office can help you check your coverage when you call 954.884.0099.

A Personal Note

I chose this work because I have seen what a thoughtful, patient centered approach can do. My own path took me through years of study here and in China, including time at the Shaolin Temple, and decades of martial arts and qigong practice. What I value most is the relationship: listening carefully, explaining clearly and working alongside you.

Questions I Encourage You to Ask Me

What do you think is going on? I will explain my assessment in both Chinese medicine and everyday terms.

What should I expect, and when? I will give you realistic goals and a time frame for reassessing.

What can I do at home? There is almost always something.

When should I see my doctor instead? I will tell you if something needs medical evaluation first.

When to Seek Medical Care First

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, a severe headache unlike any before, high fever or rapidly worsening symptoms need prompt medical care. Call 911 in an emergency.
