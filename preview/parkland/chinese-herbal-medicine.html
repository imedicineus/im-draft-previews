The Short Version

I choose classical or modified formulas based on your pattern, not only your main symptom. Granules, capsules or teas are common forms. We review medications, pregnancy status and prior reactions before anything is started.

02

Where It Tends to Help

People ask about herbs for digestion, menstrual comfort, recovery after illness, sleep, low energy and lingering pain patterns. Herbs work alongside acupuncture for many patients and sometimes stand alone when needles are not the first choice.

03

What to Expect

The first herbal visit looks a lot like an acupuncture intake: story, pulse, tongue, medications and goals. I explain the formula in plain language, how to take it and what to watch for. Follow-ups adjust the plan as your picture changes.

04

Safety Comes First

Quality and interactions matter. I use reputable sources and avoid herbs that conflict with your medicines when known risks exist. Tell me about blood thinners, blood pressure drugs, thyroid medicine, pregnancy and breastfeeding. Stop and call if you notice rash, dizziness or stomach upset that concerns you.

05

Forms I Use

Granules dissolve in warm water. Capsules are convenient for work and school. Decocted teas are less common in a busy Parkland schedule but remain an option when appropriate. I pick the form you are most likely to take correctly.

A Note on Online Herbal Products

Random internet blends are not the same as a prescribed formula. Labels can be unclear and dosing may not match your pattern. If you already take something from a store or website, bring the bottle so we can review it.

How Long Formulas Are Used

Some formulas are short courses. Others are adjusted over weeks. I do not keep people on the same bottle indefinitely without reassessment.

What the Research Says

Individual herbs and formulas have varying levels of study. I share what is known, what is traditional use and what remains uncertain so you can decide with clear eyes.

Kids, Teens and Busy Schedules

For younger patients and packed calendars, I favor simpler schedules and forms that fit real life. Taste and school routines matter as much as the textbook formula.

How Herbs Meet Acupuncture

Some Parkland patients use herbs on weeks between needle visits so support continues at home. Others begin with herbs alone when needles are not the first preference. Either path still starts with a full intake.

Tracking Response

I ask you to notice sleep, digestion, energy and the main complaint for the first one to two weeks on a formula. Those notes guide the next adjustment more than any single lab number.

Pregnancy and Nursing Caution

Many classical formulas are not appropriate in pregnancy or nursing. Always tell me before a new bottle is opened if that status has changed.

Digestion and Daily Rhythm

Parkland patients often describe bloating after rushed meals, afternoon crashes or appetite that swings with stress. Herbal plans for those pictures stay modest at first and change when your notes say the pattern shifted.

Sleep and Evening Wind Down

When sleep is part of the complaint, I look at caffeine timing, late screens and whether the formula is meant for calming, digestive ease or both. One bottle rarely fixes a chaotic evening schedule by itself.

Reassessment Points

At follow-up we decide to continue, modify or stop. Feeling better is not automatic permission to stay on the same formula forever without a fresh look.

Some people feel easier motion within a few visits. Stubborn patterns often need a longer arc. I reassess on a set schedule rather than guessing.

Yes. Visit one emphasizes listening, exam and a clear plan. Treatment may begin that day when appropriate, always with your okay.
