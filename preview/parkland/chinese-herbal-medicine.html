How I Think About Herbs

I rarely prescribe a single herb. Chinese herbal medicine works with formulas, carefully balanced combinations in which each ingredient has a job. Some address the main problem, some support them, and some temper side effects or help the body absorb the formula. Many of the formulas I use were first written down centuries ago, and they have been studied and refined by generations of practitioners since.

I choose a formula by identifying your pattern. Two people with the same complaint, say poor sleep, may get different formulas. One may feel hot, restless and irritable at night. Another may feel worn out, worried and wake at three in the morning. These are different patterns in Chinese medicine, and they call for different herbs.

03

How I Keep Herbs Safe

This is the part I take most seriously.

I review everything you take. Please bring every prescription, over the counter medicine, vitamin and supplement to your visit. Some herbs can affect blood clotting, blood sugar, blood pressure or how medications are processed. I screen every formula with that in mind.

I use quality suppliers. The NCCIH has warned that some Chinese herbal products have been contaminated or contained undeclared ingredients. I use suppliers that provide documentation of testing for identity and contaminants.

I am careful with pregnancy. Many herbs are avoided during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Please tell me if you are pregnant, trying to conceive or nursing.

I coordinate with your doctors. With your permission, I will share your formula with your physician. I also ask you to stop herbs before surgery, as your surgeon directs.

04

What the Research Says

The research on Chinese herbal medicine is growing but uneven. Some formulas have been studied in good quality trials; many have not. Reviews such as PMC7498439 discuss both promise and limitations. I will always tell you what I know about the evidence for a formula I recommend, and what I do not know.

What I Use Herbs For Most Often

Digestion. Bloating, poor appetite, irregularity and sensitive stomachs.

Sleep and stress. Trouble falling or staying asleep, worry and irritability.

Women's health. Menstrual discomfort, cycle irregularity, perimenopause and menopause, alongside gynecologic care.

Seasonal health. Formulas traditionally used at the first sign of a cold or to support recovery.

Energy. Fatigue patterns, often combined with functional medicine lab review.

Allergies. Seasonal symptoms, used together with your allergist's care.

The Forms I Use

Most of my patients take concentrated granules that dissolve in warm water. Capsules and tablets are easy for travel and for people who dislike the taste. I rarely ask anyone to cook raw herbs at home anymore, though some people enjoy the tradition.

How Long You Will Take a Formula

Typically a few weeks to a few months. I check in regularly and change the formula as you change. Herbs are not meant to be a lifelong prescription for most people.

What to Watch For

Most people do well with a carefully chosen formula. Occasionally someone has mild stomach upset or changes in bowel habits. Stop and call me if you notice a rash or feel unwell, and seek urgent care for anything severe.

Herbs and Food

In Chinese medicine, food is the first medicine. I often pair a formula with simple advice: warm cooked meals when digestion is weak, fewer cold drinks, ginger or scallion in soups when a chill sets in, congee when recovering from illness. These small changes support whatever else we do.

Herbs for Teens and Children

I treat teens with parental consent and use lower doses and simpler formulas. Some herbs are never used in children. I coordinate with your pediatrician and involve parents in every decision.

Herbs With Acupuncture

Acupuncture works while you are on the table. Herbs keep working at home every day. For many conditions, I find they work well together. You can learn more on my acupuncture page.

Questions I Encourage You to Ask Me

What do you think is going on? I will explain my assessment in both Chinese medicine and everyday terms.

What should I expect, and when? I will give you realistic goals and a time frame for reassessing.

What can I do at home? There is almost always something.

When should I see my doctor instead? I will tell you if something needs medical evaluation first.

When to Seek Medical Care First

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, a severe headache unlike any before, high fever or rapidly worsening symptoms need prompt medical care. Call 911 in an emergency.

Five Questions I Ask Before Prescribing

What else are you taking? Every medication and supplement matters.

How is your digestion? A formula has to be digested to help, so I often support digestion first.

Are you pregnant or nursing? This changes everything about herb selection.

Do you have liver or kidney concerns? These may call for extra caution or lab monitoring with your doctor.

What is realistic for you? Formulas only help if you take them, so form and dosing schedule matter.

Herbs Through South Florida's Seasons

Our long, humid summers are associated in Chinese medicine with dampness, which may show up as heaviness, sluggish digestion or low energy. Allergy seasons, school year colds and holiday travel each bring their own patterns. I adjust formulas and food advice as the seasons change.

Keeping Good Records

I record every formula's ingredients, form and dose. You are welcome to a copy for your records or your other doctors.

What I Hope You Take Away

Good care is a partnership. I will listen carefully, explain what I find, tell you honestly what the evidence says and give you practical steps you can take at home. You bring your questions, your goals and your feedback. Together, we adjust until the plan fits.

Herbs and Teens' Busy Lives

Teens who skip meals, sleep too little and carry heavy schedules often show patterns of fatigue and stress. When appropriate, I keep formulas simple and easy to take, and I pair them with basic habits: breakfast, water, bedtime. Parents are always part of the conversation.

A Word on Online Herbal Products

I understand the appeal of ordering herbs online. But products vary widely in quality and accuracy of labeling, and combining them with medications without guidance can be risky. If you already take an herbal product, bring it to your visit and I will review it with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take herbs with my prescriptions?

Often yes, after I screen for interactions. Never stop a prescription without your doctor.

Do herbs taste bad?

Some do. Capsules avoid the taste entirely.

Are herbs regulated?

In the U.S., they are regulated as dietary supplements, which is why sourcing matters.

Will I need acupuncture too?

Not necessarily. Some people use herbs alone.
