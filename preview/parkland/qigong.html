The Short Version

You learn slow postures, easy breathing and attention to balance. Sessions can be private, small group or woven into a treatment visit. The aim is steadier energy, less guarding in the body and a practice you can repeat at home in a few minutes.

02

Where It Tends to Help

People use qigong for stress, stiff necks and backs, balance confidence, recovery between harder training days and a calmer evening routine. It is complementary care, not a substitute for medical treatment of disease.

03

What to Expect

I watch stance, breath and tension in the shoulders and jaw. Corrections are small and practical. You leave with a short routine you can do in a living room or on a quiet path in a Parkland park.

04

A Routine to Begin With

Most beginners do well with five to ten minutes of standing, arm floats and easy weight shifts. Consistency beats intensity. If a movement hurts, we change it.

05

How Qigong Fits With Treatment

Needle visits address tissue and pattern in the clinic. Qigong keeps the nervous system and joints practicing ease between visits. Many patients combine both.

What the Research Says

Mind body movement research, including tai chi and qigong style practices, reports benefits for balance, stress and some pain related quality of life measures in selected groups. Study designs vary, so I present findings carefully.

Different Seasons of Life

Teens, busy parents and older adults can all practice with adjustments. Chairs and shorter sets are always fine.

The Three Things I Watch For

Soft knees, easy breath and shoulders that are not braced up by the ears. Get those right and the rest of the form improves quickly.

Outdoor Practice in Parkland

Quiet park paths work well for standing sets when weather cooperates. Shade, flat ground and shoes you trust matter more than finding a perfect studio.

Building a Habit

Link practice to something you already do, such as after morning coffee or before evening screens. Tiny daily sets outperform rare long ones.

When to Modify

Knee irritation, dizziness or recent surgery mean we simplify stance and range. Pride has no place in a rehab minded practice.

Breath Before Complexity

If standing postures feel like too much on a given day, we begin with seated breath and shoulder drops. Complexity returns only when ease is stable.

Teaching in the Community

Years of park and event teaching in Parkland taught me that clear cues beat fancy choreography. Patients hear the same plain language in clinic that students hear outdoors.

Measuring Progress Honestly

Easier mornings, fewer braced shoulders and a practice you actually repeat are better success markers than perfect form photos.
