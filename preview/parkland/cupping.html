How It Feels

Massage presses down on muscles. Cupping lifts them. I place cups on the skin and create gentle suction, either with a hand pump or a brief flame inside a glass cup. The flame never touches your skin. Most people describe a deep, pulling stretch that feels good on tight areas. I adjust suction to your comfort.

03

The Ways I Use Cups

Stationary. Cups stay in one place for five to ten minutes.

Moving. With a little oil, I glide a cup along the muscle.

Flash. Quick placement and release, gentle and lighter on marks.

With acupuncture. Cups placed near or over treated areas.

04

About the Marks

Cups often leave round pink, red or purple marks from small capillaries releasing a little blood near the surface. They usually do not hurt and fade over about one to two weeks. If you have a wedding, beach day or photos coming up, let me know and I will use lighter techniques.

05

What the Research Says

According to the NCCIH, some research suggests cupping may help with pain, including neck and low back pain, but many studies are low quality and more research is needed. I offer cupping as a supportive therapy within a broader plan.

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Who I Commonly Use It For

Athletes with sore, tight muscles. Parents and desk workers with upper back and shoulder tension. People who carry stress in their shoulders and neck.

A Little History

Cupping has been used in China, Egypt, Greece and the Middle East for centuries. Early cups were made from animal horn, bamboo or clay. The basic idea, gentle suction to lift and loosen tissue, has stayed the same.

When I Avoid Cupping

I do not use cupping over broken skin, sunburn, rashes, varicose veins, fresh injuries or areas of swelling. Please tell me if you are pregnant, take blood thinners or bruise easily.

Aftercare

Keep the treated area covered and warm. Drink water. Avoid hot tubs, saunas and strong sun on the area for the rest of the day, and keep exercise light until tomorrow.

Pairing With Other Care

I often combine cupping with acupuncture, gua sha and dry needling.

Questions I Encourage You to Ask Me

What do you think is going on? I will explain my assessment in both Chinese medicine and everyday terms.

What should I expect, and when? I will give you realistic goals and a time frame for reassessing.

What can I do at home? There is almost always something.

When should I see my doctor instead? I will tell you if something needs medical evaluation first.

When to Seek Medical Care First

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness or numbness, a severe headache unlike any before, high fever or rapidly worsening symptoms need prompt medical care. Call 911 in an emergency.
