4. How the First Visit Runs

Intake. Dr. Fields reviews your history, medications, labs and goals.

Assessment. He checks posture, range of motion and tender areas, along with the traditional pulse and tongue observation used in Chinese medicine.

Treatment. Thin, sterile, single use needles are placed at selected points. Most people feel a light tap, then a dull ache or warmth. You rest for about 20 to 30 minutes.

Plan. Before you leave, you receive a schedule and one or two simple things to do at home.

5. How Many Sessions?

Short term problems sometimes change within a handful of visits. Long standing issues usually need more time. A common pattern is weekly visits for several weeks, then a reassessment. We would rather review results honestly than keep you coming without a clear reason.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

How Researchers Think Acupuncture Works

Western science has proposed several mechanisms. Needling stimulates nerves in muscle and connective tissue, which send signals to the spinal cord and brain. Studies suggest this may influence how the nervous system processes pain and may involve the body's own pain modulating chemistry. Researchers have also examined the role of fascia, the connective tissue wrapping muscles, in how needle stimulation is transmitted (Langevin et al., FASEB Journal, 2001). These models are still being studied, and no single explanation is settled.

The Chinese Medicine View

Classical theory describes channels through which qi and blood circulate. Pain is understood as something that is stuck, and fatigue as something depleted. Dr. Fields uses both languages. The traditional framework guides point selection and lifestyle advice, while the anatomical view keeps the plan grounded in how muscles, joints and nerves behave.

What the Evidence Suggests

A large individual patient data meta-analysis published in the Journal of Pain pooled trials on chronic pain and found acupuncture performed better than sham and no acupuncture control groups, with effects that persisted over time for many patients (Vickers et al., 2018). The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health summarizes research on acupuncture for low back pain, neck pain, knee osteoarthritis and headaches. Results differ by person and condition, and research continues.

Safety

When performed by a licensed, trained practitioner using sterile single use needles, acupuncture is generally considered safe. Mild bruising, soreness or brief lightheadedness can occur. Tell us if you take blood thinners, have a bleeding disorder, use a pacemaker or are pregnant, so treatment can be adjusted.

Working With Your Physician

Acupuncture works well as part of a broader plan. Keep your medical doctor informed and continue prescribed medications unless your prescriber advises otherwise. With your permission, Dr. Fields can share notes with your other providers.

Making Results Last

The treatment table is only part of the plan. Between visits, small changes add up: a short walk after lunch, a screen cutoff an hour before bed, a stretch break every 90 minutes at your desk and adequate hydration in the Florida heat. Dr. Fields keeps the homework short because busy people follow short lists.

Related Treatments

Depending on your case, Dr. Fields may combine acupuncture with Chinese herbal medicine, dry needling, cupping, gua sha or functional medicine testing. Many patients also learn a short qigong routine.

Acupuncture for Desk and Travel Strain

Long flights out of PBIA, hours in client meetings and evenings answering email from the couch all load the same structures: the neck, the upper back, the hip flexors and the low back. Dr. Fields looks at how you sit, stand and carry a bag, then targets the muscles that take the brunt. Points in the neck and shoulders address local tension, while distal points on the hands and legs are chosen according to Chinese medicine theory. Many patients also get a two minute stretch sequence designed for airport gates and conference rooms.

Sleep and Stress

Stress is a common reason professionals first book, even when pain is what brought them in. Racing thoughts at night, shallow breathing and a jaw clenched through the workday are patterns Dr. Fields sees often. Acupuncture sessions are quiet and screen free by design, and many patients find that hour of rest valuable in itself. Research on acupuncture for insomnia and anxiety is ongoing, with some encouraging but mixed results, so we track sleep and stress over time rather than promising an outcome.

Headaches and Migraines

Tension type headaches often trace back to tight neck and shoulder muscles, while migraines have more complex triggers involving sleep, hormones, food and stress. Dr. Fields asks you to keep a simple headache log so patterns become visible. Acupuncture for migraine prevention has been studied in several clinical trials, and the NCCIH notes that it may help reduce the frequency of some types of headaches. Your neurologist or primary care physician should stay involved, particularly for new or changing headaches.

Women's Health and Fertility Support

Menstrual pain, irregular cycles, perimenopausal symptoms and support during fertility treatment are common reasons women in Boca Raton seek acupuncture. Dr. Fields coordinates timing with your cycle or your fertility clinic's schedule when needed and keeps your reproductive endocrinologist informed if you wish. Acupuncture is offered as support, not a replacement for medical fertility care.

Joint Pain in Active Adults

Knees after years of tennis, ankles after old sprains, elbows irritated by golf: joint complaints are common in an active community like ours. Knee osteoarthritis is one of the better studied areas for acupuncture. Dr. Fields pairs needling with strength and mobility suggestions, because joints usually do better when the surrounding muscles are strong.

What to Track

To make results measurable, we ask you to rate your main symptom from 0 to 10 at each visit and note sleep quality, medication use and activity. Numbers help both of us decide whether to continue, change direction or stop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does acupuncture hurt?

The needles are much thinner than injection needles. Most people feel a brief pinch or a dull ache, and many find the session relaxing.

Can I go back to work after a session?

Most people can. Some feel very relaxed, so plan a calm hour if you can.

Do you take insurance?

Coverage varies by plan. Call the office and we will explain your options.

How should I prepare?

Eat a light meal beforehand, wear loose clothing and bring a list of medications and any recent lab work.

Is it safe during pregnancy?

Some points are avoided during pregnancy. Tell us if you are or might be pregnant and check with your obstetrician.
