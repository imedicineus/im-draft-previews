Gua Sha in Boca Raton

Gua sha has become a social media skin care trend, but the clinical version is something different: a firm, repeated stroke across muscle with a smooth tool to address stiffness and pain. In our Boca Raton office, Dr. Jonathan M. Fields uses it most for the neck, shoulders and upper back.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Clinical Gua Sha Versus Facial Gua Sha

Facial gua sha uses light pressure and is a cosmetic routine. Clinical gua sha uses more pressure on the body and is done by a trained practitioner as part of musculoskeletal or traditional care. Both use similar tools, but the goals differ.

What the Marks Mean

The redness and small dots come from tiny capillaries releasing blood near the surface. They are not the same as a bruise from a blow. They typically fade within two to four days.

What Research Suggests

Small studies have looked at gua sha for neck pain and other musculoskeletal complaints, and some have observed short term improvement in pain or movement. Researchers have also studied changes in local skin blood flow after treatment. The overall body of research is limited, so Dr. Fields presents gua sha as a supportive option rather than a stand alone fix.

Instrument Assisted Soft Tissue Work

Physical therapists and athletic trainers use a related family of techniques called instrument assisted soft tissue mobilization. The tools and terminology differ, but the idea of using a firm edge on soft tissue is shared.

Who Should Skip It

Avoid gua sha over broken skin, rashes, recent injuries, varicose veins or areas of active inflammation. Tell us if you take blood thinners, have a bleeding disorder or are pregnant.

Pairing Options

Gua sha is often combined with acupuncture, cupping and dry needling.

Gua Sha and Tech Neck

Hours looking down at phones and laptops pull the head forward and load the muscles at the base of the skull and between the shoulder blades. Gua sha over those areas, combined with chin tucks and a better monitor height, is a common plan for professionals. Dr. Fields usually gives you two or three simple drills to support the treatment.

How Many Sessions?

Many people try a short series, then use gua sha occasionally as needed.

Tools We Use

Clinical tools are made from materials such as jade, stainless steel or horn, and each has a smooth, rounded edge. Every tool is cleaned and disinfected between patients, and a light oil or balm is used to allow the stroke to glide.

Gua Sha During a Cold

In traditional practice, gua sha on the upper back and neck is used at the first sign of a cold with chills and stiffness. This is a traditional use, and it does not replace medical care for fever, shortness of breath or worsening symptoms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is gua sha painful?

It should not be. Pressure can be adjusted at any time.

Can I go to work afterward?

Yes. Marks on the back are easy to cover.

How often can I have it?

Usually not until marks from the prior session have faded.
