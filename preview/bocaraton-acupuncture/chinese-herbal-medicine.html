2. Why Formulas, Not Single Herbs

In Western supplements, one product often targets one goal. Chinese medicine rarely works that way. A typical formula has a lead herb aimed at the main problem, supporting herbs that broaden its action, herbs that offset side effects and an herb that harmonizes the group. That structure has been refined over roughly two thousand years of written texts and clinical commentary.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

A Living Pharmacopeia

The Chinese materia medica includes hundreds of commonly used substances, most of them plant roots, barks, seeds, flowers and fungi. Each is described by temperature (warming or cooling), taste and the organ systems it is thought to influence. Ginger is warming and is used for nausea and chills. Mint is cooling and is used at the start of a cold. Those simple categories help a practitioner balance a formula so it does not overheat or overcool the body.

Patterns Versus Diagnoses

Two people with the same Western diagnosis, say insomnia, can receive very different formulas. One may be described as having heat disturbing the mind, with racing thoughts and night sweats. Another may be described as depleted, waking at 3 a.m. exhausted. Treating the pattern rather than the label is the core idea of Chinese medicine.

Modern Research

Researchers have studied many traditional herbs and formulas, and some compounds derived from Chinese herbs have become important medicines. The best known is artemisinin, isolated from sweet wormwood, whose discovery was recognized with the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. A review in the National Library of Medicine discusses evidence and challenges in herbal research (PMC7498439). The NCCIH also offers a balanced overview, including safety concerns. Overall, the evidence is mixed and varies by herb and condition.

Drug Interactions

Herbs are biologically active, which means they can interact with medications. Blood thinners, diabetes drugs, blood pressure medicines, thyroid hormone and immune suppressing drugs deserve particular attention. Always disclose everything you take, including over the counter supplements, and keep your physician informed.

Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Many herbs are avoided during pregnancy and breastfeeding. If you are pregnant, trying to conceive or nursing, tell us at the start so the plan reflects that.

Forms and Dosing

Granules dissolve in hot water and are easy to carry to the office. Capsules and tablets are convenient for travel. Traditional decoctions, cooked from raw herbs, are less common today but are still used for some cases. Dosing is set by Dr. Fields and adjusted as needed, and you should not change it without asking.

Herbs in a Combined Plan

Formulas often pair with acupuncture and functional medicine testing. Lab results can help guide which systems to support and show whether changes are happening over time.

A Look at Classical Formulas

Many formulas in daily use are centuries old. Xiao Yao San, often translated as Free and Easy Wanderer, is a classical formula associated with stress, irritability and digestive upset. Gui Pi Tang is traditionally used for fatigue with poor sleep and worry. Si Jun Zi Tang, the Four Gentlemen, is a foundational formula for weak digestion and low energy. Dr. Fields uses these as starting points, adding or removing herbs to fit the individual. Their names and traditional uses are descriptions from the classical literature, not evidence that they will help a specific person.

Herbs for Stress and Sleep

High pressure careers tend to produce a recognizable pattern in Chinese medicine: tension in the neck and jaw, irritability, digestion that worsens with stress and sleep that is light or broken. Formulas aimed at that pattern are often among the most requested at our Boca Raton office. Dr. Fields pairs them with practical sleep strategies, because no formula can outpace a habit of answering email at midnight.

Seasonal and Travel Support

Frequent travelers sometimes ask for formulas to keep on hand for the start of a cold or for digestive upset on the road. Dr. Fields can discuss what makes sense for your situation, along with clear guidance on when symptoms call for a visit to urgent care or your physician instead.

Women's Health Formulas

Herbal medicine has a long history in gynecology. Formulas are commonly used for menstrual discomfort, irregular cycles and menopausal symptoms such as hot flashes and night sweats. If you are working with a fertility clinic or are taking hormone therapy, Dr. Fields will coordinate carefully and may recommend avoiding certain herbs during specific phases of treatment.

How We Measure Progress

At each follow up, you rate your main symptoms and report any side effects. Digestive changes, headaches or rashes are reasons to call before your next visit. If a formula is not helping after a reasonable trial, Dr. Fields changes it rather than continuing indefinitely.

What Herbal Medicine Is Not

Herbal formulas are not a replacement for antibiotics in a bacterial infection, insulin in type 1 diabetes or other essential medical care. They are one tool within a broader plan, used thoughtfully and with full disclosure to your other providers.

Storage and Travel

Keep granules and capsules in a cool, dry place, away from the humidity of a Florida bathroom. When traveling, carry them in original labeled containers along with a copy of your formula.

Questions to Ask Any Herbalist

Ask where herbs are sourced, how they are tested, what each herb in your formula is for and how long you should take it. A good practitioner welcomes those questions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do the herbs taste bad?

Some are bitter. Capsules avoid the taste entirely.

How long will I take a formula?

It depends on the goal. Short term formulas may last a week or two, while chronic patterns are reassessed every few weeks.

Can I take herbs with my prescriptions?

Sometimes. Dr. Fields reviews your list first and may suggest timing or changes. Do not stop prescribed medicine without your prescriber's guidance.

Are herbs regulated?

In the U.S., herbal products are regulated as dietary supplements. That is why supplier testing matters.

Can children take herbs?

Sometimes, with adjusted doses and parental consent. Ask Dr. Fields about your child's situation.
