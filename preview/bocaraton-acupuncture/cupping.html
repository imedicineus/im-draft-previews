The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

A Brief History

Cupping appears in traditional medicine across many cultures, including Chinese, Middle Eastern and European folk practice. Early cups were made from animal horn or bamboo. Today most clinics use glass with a flame to create suction or plastic cups with a hand pump.

About the Marks

The color comes from small capillaries near the surface releasing blood under suction. Darker marks are common in tight or less mobile areas. They are not bruises from impact, and they are usually painless. In Chinese medicine, the color is sometimes read as information about circulation in the area.

Static Versus Moving Cups

Static cupping leaves cups in place for several minutes. Moving cupping glides cups along oiled muscle, more like a massage stroke. Dr. Fields may use either, depending on the area and your goals.

Research

Studies on cupping have looked mainly at neck and back pain. Some trials suggest short term improvements in pain, but many are small or have design limits, and more rigorous research is needed. The NCCIH provides an overview of current evidence and safety.

Precautions

Cupping is avoided over broken skin, varicose veins, recent injuries and areas of active inflammation. Tell us if you take blood thinners, bruise easily, have a bleeding disorder or are pregnant.

Combining Therapies

Cupping pairs naturally with acupuncture, dry needling and gua sha for musculoskeletal complaints.

Cupping for Golf, Tennis and Pickleball

Repetitive swings and quick lateral movement tighten the back, hips and shoulders. Many active Boca Raton patients use cupping as part of their recovery routine during busy seasons of play. Share your match or tee time schedule so sessions can be timed well.

What Dr. Fields Looks For

Before placing cups, he checks posture, the range of motion of your neck, shoulders and hips and where tissue feels thick or restricted. That assessment decides cup placement, suction strength and whether moving or static cups make more sense.

Cupping and Acupuncture in One Visit

A common sequence is acupuncture first, with you lying face down, followed by cups on the back. The combination usually adds only a few minutes and lets Dr. Fields address both the local tightness and the broader pattern.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does cupping hurt?

It should not. Tell Dr. Fields if suction feels too strong.

How long do the marks last?

Usually several days to about a week or so, depending on the person and area.

Can I have cupping on its own?

It is usually part of a broader treatment, but ask about your situation.

How often?

Often once a week during a course of care, allowing marks to fade between sessions.
