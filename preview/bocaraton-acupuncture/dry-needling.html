3. A Session, Step by Step

Dr. Fields palpates the area to locate tight bands and tender points, then inserts a needle into the trigger point. You may feel a quick twitch or a deep ache that reproduces your familiar pain. Needles are removed promptly or left briefly, then range of motion is rechecked. Most sessions end with stretches or corrective exercises to do at home.

What Is a Trigger Point?

A trigger point is a hyperirritable spot within a taut band of muscle. It can be tender to the touch and may refer pain to other areas; a trigger point in the upper trapezius, for example, is commonly linked to pain at the temple. Overuse, posture, injury and stress are frequent contributors.

Dry Needling and Acupuncture

Dry needling grew out of Western trigger point research, while acupuncture comes from Chinese medicine. Dr. Fields is trained in both. In practice, this means he can use trigger point techniques for a stubborn muscle and broader acupuncture for sleep, stress or whole body balance in the same plan.

How It May Work

Researchers think needling a trigger point may reduce local muscle tension, change chemical activity in the area and alter nerve signaling related to pain. The classic gate control theory of pain (Melzack and Wall, 1965) is one of several frameworks used to discuss how sensory input can modulate pain. Studies on dry needling have shown promising results for some conditions, though trial quality varies.

Who Should Avoid It

Dry needling may not be suitable if you have a bleeding disorder, take blood thinners, have a local infection or are in the first trimester of pregnancy. Tell us about implants, recent surgery or a fear of needles before your visit.

Pairing With Other Care

Some patients benefit from adding cupping or gua sha to work on larger areas of tight tissue.

Dry Needling for Golfers and Tennis Players

Rotational sports load the spine, hips and shoulders unevenly. Trigger points in the rotator cuff, forearm extensors, obliques and glutes are common in players at Boca's many clubs. Dr. Fields assesses your swing related pain patterns and works with you on mobility drills, so the treatment supports how you actually play.

How Many Visits?

Acute muscle problems often need only a few sessions. Chronic patterns, especially those tied to posture or repetitive work, usually need a short series plus exercises. Dr. Fields reassesses every few visits.

Your Exercise Plan

Needling opens a window of reduced tension. Stretching and strengthening help keep it open, so expect a short list of exercises tailored to the area treated.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does it hurt?

The twitch can feel intense for a second. Most people describe the discomfort as brief and tolerable.

How soon will I notice a difference?

Some people feel looser right away. Others notice change over several sessions.

Can I work out afterward?

Light activity is fine. Save heavy training for the next day.
