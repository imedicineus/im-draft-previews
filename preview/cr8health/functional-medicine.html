The Core Idea

Two people with the same diagnosis can have very different reasons for it. Functional medicine asks why. Fatigue might trace back to iron status, thyroid function, sleep quality, blood sugar swings or a combination. By mapping these factors, the plan can be more specific than a generic recommendation.

The Process

1. Intake. A long first visit covers your timeline, diet, sleep, stress, environment, medications and family history.

2. Data. Dr. Fields reviews existing labs and may suggest additional tests to discuss with your physician.

3. Analysis. Blood chemistry is interpreted as a pattern, comparing markers with each other and over time.

4. Plan. You receive a prioritized plan with clear first steps, usually focused on nutrition, sleep and movement before supplements.

5. Review. Follow up visits track symptoms and repeat labs at sensible intervals.

The deeper story

For readers who want the details: the research, the history and the reasoning behind how we work.

Blood Chemistry Analysis

A standard panel often contains more information than a quick normal or abnormal reading reveals. Dr. Fields studied blood chemistry analysis and looks at relationships, such as how iron, ferritin and transferrin saturation fit together, or how glucose, A1c and triglycerides relate. Findings are always discussed with your physician, who remains responsible for medical diagnosis.

Thyroid

Fatigue, weight change, feeling cold, dry skin and constipation can relate to thyroid function. A fuller thyroid picture may include TSH, free T4, free T3 and antibodies. Autoimmune thyroid conditions such as Hashimoto's are common, and nutrition and lifestyle factors are often part of the conversation alongside endocrine care.

Blood Sugar and Metabolic Health

Fasting glucose, A1c, triglycerides, HDL and waist measurement give a picture of metabolic health. Meal composition, protein and fiber intake, sleep, stress and muscle strength all influence these markers. Plans are practical and built around your schedule and food preferences.

Digestive Health

Bloating, reflux, irregularity and food reactions can affect energy, mood and nutrient status. Dr. Fields looks at diet, eating habits, medications and stress. An elimination and reintroduction approach may be used for suspected food triggers, with care to keep the diet balanced.

Hormonal Changes

Perimenopause, menopause and age related changes in men can bring sleep disruption, mood changes, weight shifts and lower energy. Functional medicine looks at nutrition, stress and sleep, while hormone therapy decisions stay with your physician. Acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine may also be part of the plan.

Inflammation

Chronic low grade inflammation is associated with many conditions. Diet quality, body composition, sleep, activity and blood sugar control all influence inflammatory markers. Plans emphasize whole foods, fiber, healthy fats and movement.

Supplements With a Reason

Supplements are recommended when there is a clear reason, such as a documented deficiency, and doses are chosen with your medications in mind. Dr. Fields often simplifies regimens for people who arrive taking many products.

What the Evidence Says

Some parts of functional medicine, such as improving diet, correcting deficiencies, regular exercise and sleep, have strong support. Some specialty tests and supplements have less. Dr. Fields explains the evidence behind each recommendation and focuses first on approaches with the best support.

Pairing With Acupuncture

Functional medicine often works alongside acupuncture for pain, stress, sleep and digestive symptoms.

What a First Visit Covers

Your timeline. When did you last feel well? What changed around the time symptoms began: a move, a new job, an illness, a medication, a pregnancy or a loss?

Nutrition. A typical day of eating, including meal timing, protein, vegetables, alcohol, caffeine and snacks.

Sleep. Bedtime, wake time, awakenings, snoring and how rested you feel.

Movement. Exercise type, frequency and how your body responds.

Stress and recovery. Workload, relationships, time outdoors and practices that help you recover.

Environment. Occupational exposures, mold or water damage at home, and other relevant factors.

Common Patterns We See

Tired but wired. Poor sleep, reliance on caffeine and evening energy spikes, often tied to stress and irregular meals.

Afternoon crash. Energy drops after lunch, sometimes associated with meal composition and blood sugar.

Gut and mood. Digestive symptoms that rise and fall with stress.

Midlife shift. Changes in sleep, weight and mood during perimenopause or andropause.

Normal labs, not feeling normal. Results within reference ranges but a pattern worth exploring further.

Food First

Dr. Fields builds plans around real meals. Typical starting points include protein at breakfast, more vegetables and fiber, fewer ultra processed foods, consistent meal timing and adequate hydration. Plans respect cultural foods, budgets and schedules. For travelers and busy professionals, he focuses on practical options rather than perfect ones.

Sleep and Light

Consistent sleep and wake times, morning light exposure and a wind down routine are among the most effective changes many people can make. Snoring, gasping at night or excessive daytime sleepiness should be evaluated by a physician for sleep apnea.

Movement as Medicine

Both strength training and aerobic activity improve insulin sensitivity, mood and sleep. Dr. Fields suggests starting points that fit your current fitness and may include qigong for stress and mobility.

Tracking Outcomes

Each plan sets a few markers to follow, such as energy rating, sleep quality, digestive symptoms, waist measurement and selected lab values. Progress is reviewed at follow up so the plan reflects what is actually working.

Common Labs Reviewed

Depending on your history, a review may include a complete blood count, comprehensive metabolic panel, lipid panel, A1c, fasting insulin, thyroid panel, ferritin and iron studies, vitamin D, B12 and folate, and inflammatory markers. Not everyone needs every test. Dr. Fields prioritizes the ones most likely to change decisions and discusses ordering with you and your physician.

Why Patterns Beat Single Numbers

A single result just inside or outside a reference range can be misleading. Trends over time, and relationships among markers, often tell a clearer story. For example, rising fasting glucose and triglycerides with falling HDL over several years may point toward insulin resistance before diabetes develops. Seeing the trend early creates time to act.

When to See Your Physician First

Unexplained weight loss, chest pain, shortness of breath, blood in the stool, new neurological symptoms or severe fatigue need prompt medical evaluation. Functional medicine is not a substitute for urgent or diagnostic care.

No. It complements medical care and is coordinated with your physicians.

Do I need new lab tests?

Not always. Existing labs are reviewed first.

How long before I notice changes?

Many people notice early changes within several weeks of diet and sleep adjustments. Lab changes often take a few months.
