Traditional Roots, Modern Cousins

Gua sha comes from Chinese folk and clinical practice. Physical therapists use a related approach called instrument assisted soft tissue mobilization. The tools and goals overlap, though the traditional framework differs.

03

The Technique

Preparation. Skin is checked and lightly oiled.

Strokes. Short, unidirectional strokes along the muscle with steady pressure.

Endpoint. Treatment stops when color appears or the tissue softens.

Movement. You move the area to feel the change.

04

Research Context

Small clinical studies have reported short term improvements in neck and back pain after gua sha, and laboratory work has measured increased surface microcirculation in treated areas. Study sizes are small and more research is needed. Gua sha is offered as a supportive therapy within a larger plan.

Facial Gua Sha Is Different

Cosmetic facial gua sha uses very light pressure for relaxation and skin care. Clinical gua sha on the body uses more pressure and has different goals. Our focus is clinical musculoskeletal care.

Who Should Avoid Gua Sha

Skip it over broken skin, rashes, sunburn, moles, varicose veins, fresh injuries or areas of swelling. Tell us about blood thinners, bleeding disorders or pregnancy.

Aftercare

Keep the area covered and warm, hydrate and avoid saunas, hot tubs and strong sun for the rest of the day.

Combining Approaches

Gua sha is often used with cupping, acupuncture and home mobility work.

Understanding Sha

The red dots that appear during gua sha are called petechiae, or sha in Chinese. They result from small amounts of blood leaving capillaries near the surface. In traditional practice, the color and how quickly it fades are noted as part of assessment. The marks are typically painless and do not indicate injury to deeper tissue.

Tools and Hygiene

We use smooth tools made of stainless steel, stone or ceramic. Tools are cleaned and disinfected between every patient, and treatment surfaces are covered with fresh linens.

Gua Sha and Mobility

Gua sha is often followed by stretching or mobility drills. Softening tight tissue first may make these exercises feel easier. For neck and shoulder tension, a few minutes of gua sha followed by gentle range of motion work is a common sequence.

Home Care Between Visits

Use heat for tight muscles, take regular breaks from sitting, sleep with a supportive pillow and keep your workstation at a comfortable height. Simple stretches, such as doorway chest stretches and gentle neck turns, help maintain progress.

Gua Sha During Colds

In traditional Chinese practice, gua sha on the upper back and neck is used at the start of a cold with chills and stiffness. This is a traditional use rather than a researched treatment. See your physician for fever, breathing difficulty or symptoms that do not improve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does gua sha hurt?

It may feel intense in tight spots, but pressure is adjusted to your comfort.

How long do marks last?

Usually two to four days.

How often can I have it?

Usually not more than once a week on the same area.
