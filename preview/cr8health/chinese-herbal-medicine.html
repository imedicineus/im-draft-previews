A Formula, Not a Single Herb

Chinese herbal medicine rarely uses one herb alone. Formulas combine several ingredients that work together, with some addressing the main pattern, some supporting the others and some balancing side effects. Classical formulas have been recorded and refined over centuries, and Dr. Fields adjusts them to fit the individual.

03

How a Formula Is Chosen

Pattern diagnosis. Chinese medicine groups symptoms into patterns. Two people with insomnia may receive different formulas because one runs hot and restless and the other is tired and anxious.

Constitution and context. Age, digestion, medications and other conditions shape the choice.

Form and dose. Granules mixed with water are common. Capsules and tablets are convenient for travel and busy schedules.

Review. Formulas are reassessed at follow up and changed as you change.

Research Context

Chinese herbal medicine is studied around the world, though research quality varies and results depend on the formula and condition. The NCCIH notes that some Chinese herbal products have been contaminated or contained undeclared ingredients, which is why sourcing matters. Reviews such as PMC7498439 discuss both potential benefits and the need for careful study.

Common Reasons People Use Herbs

Digestion. Bloating, irregularity and poor appetite.

Sleep and stress. Formulas for restlessness, worry and waking at night.

Women's health. Menstrual discomfort, cycle irregularity and menopausal symptoms, coordinated with gynecologic care.

Seasonal support. Formulas used at the first signs of a cold, and for recovery afterward.

Energy. Fatigue patterns, often alongside functional medicine lab review.

Skin. Some skin conditions, with dermatology care.

Interaction Screening

Some herbs can affect blood clotting, blood sugar, blood pressure or how the liver processes drugs. Dr. Fields screens every formula against your medication list and asks you to report new prescriptions. People taking blood thinners, diabetes medications, immunosuppressants or many medications receive extra attention. Formulas are typically paused before surgery, following your surgeon's guidance.

Quality and Sourcing

We use suppliers that provide documentation of testing for identity and contaminants such as heavy metals and pesticides. Formulas sold online without documentation are not recommended.

Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Many herbs are avoided during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Tell us if you are pregnant, trying to conceive or nursing.

Working With Functional Medicine

Herbal formulas and functional medicine can complement each other. Labs and nutrition address measurable factors while formulas address the overall pattern. Dr. Fields avoids duplicating ingredients between herbs and supplements.

Working With Acupuncture

Herbs work between visits, while acupuncture works during them. Many plans use both.

Classical Formulas You May Hear About

Dr. Fields may mention formula names during your visit. A few examples from the classical literature:

Xiao Yao San (Free and Easy Wanderer). A classical formula often used in patterns involving stress, irritability, digestive upset and menstrual irregularity.

Gui Pi Tang (Restore the Spleen Decoction). Traditionally used in patterns of fatigue, poor appetite, worry and restless sleep.

Liu Wei Di Huang Wan (Six Ingredient Pill with Rehmannia). A foundational formula in patterns described as yin deficiency, sometimes associated with night sweats, dryness or heat sensations.

Yu Ping Feng San (Jade Windscreen Powder). Traditionally used to support resistance in people who catch colds easily.

These names describe traditional uses and patterns, not guaranteed outcomes. Formulas are always modified for the individual.

Granules, Pills or Raw Herbs

Granules. Concentrated extracts that dissolve in warm water. They allow custom formulas and are the most common form we use.

Capsules and tablets. Convenient and tasteless, usually prepared from standardized classical formulas.

Raw herbs. Cooked into a decoction at home. This is the most traditional method, but it takes time and the smell can be strong. It is used less often today.

What to Watch For

Report any new digestive upset, rash, headache or change in how you feel. Most people tolerate well chosen formulas, and dose or ingredients can be adjusted. Stop and call us if you notice signs of an allergic reaction, and seek urgent care for severe symptoms.

Herbs and Lab Monitoring

For people on long courses of herbal medicine, or with liver or kidney concerns, Dr. Fields may suggest periodic lab monitoring with your physician. This is part of a careful, data informed approach that fits the CR8 Health model.

Children and Older Adults

Doses are adjusted for age, body size and overall health. Older adults taking many medications receive particularly careful interaction screening. Children are treated only with appropriate formulas and with parental involvement.

How CR8 Health Documents Herbal Care

Every formula is recorded with its ingredients, form and dose. If you see another clinician, you can share this list. When you ask us to, we will send it directly to your physician.

Seasonal Patterns in South Florida

Our climate shapes common complaints. Humid summers are associated in Chinese medicine with dampness patterns, which may show up as heaviness, sluggish digestion or fatigue. Air conditioning, travel and allergy seasons bring their own challenges. Formulas and dietary advice are adapted to the season and to how you are actually feeling.

Herbs Versus Supplements

Western supplements usually deliver a single nutrient or extract at a set dose. Chinese formulas combine several ingredients chosen to address an overall pattern. The two can be used together when there is a clear plan, but stacking many products without oversight increases the risk of interactions and wasted money. Dr. Fields reviews everything you take and often consolidates.

Starting a Formula: Practical Tips

Take it with consistency. Most formulas are taken two or three times a day. Link doses to meals or another daily habit.

Note changes. Keep brief notes on sleep, digestion, energy and the symptom you are treating.

Travel. Capsules or single dose granule packets are easiest for trips.

Storage. Keep granules dry and sealed. Humidity in Florida can cause clumping.

Running out. Call a few days ahead so there is no gap if the formula is going well.

When Herbs Are Not the Right Choice

Herbs may not be appropriate for people with certain liver or kidney conditions, those on some high risk medications, or in pregnancy. Sometimes acupuncture, nutrition or a referral is a better fit. Dr. Fields will tell you when he thinks herbs should wait or be avoided.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do herbs taste bad?

Some do. Capsules and tablets avoid the taste.

How long will I take a formula?

Often a few weeks to a few months, with regular review.

Can I take herbs with my prescriptions?

Often yes, after screening. Never stop a prescription without your doctor.

Are herbs regulated?

In the United States, they are regulated as dietary supplements, which is why quality sourcing matters.
