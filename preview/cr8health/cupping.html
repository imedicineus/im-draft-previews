Why Suction Feels Different

Massage presses tissue down. Cupping lifts it. Many people describe the sensation as a deep stretch or pulling that eases tight areas. Gentle movement under the cups can also help areas feel looser afterward.

03

Methods We Use

Stationary cupping. Cups rest in one place for several minutes.

Moving cupping. Oiled skin allows a cup to glide along muscle.

Flash cupping. Quick applications and releases, gentle and lighter marking.

Cups with acupuncture. Cups may be placed over areas treated with needles.

04

What the Evidence Says

According to the NCCIH, some research suggests cupping may help with pain, including neck and low back pain, but many studies are of low quality, and more rigorous research is needed. Side effects can include discoloration, bruising and, rarely, burns or skin infection. We present cupping as a supportive therapy within a broader plan.

About the Marks

Suction draws blood from small capillaries toward the surface, producing circular marks. They usually do not hurt. Color tends to vary between people and areas. Plan ahead if you have an event where your back or shoulders will be visible.

Cupping for Training Recovery

Athletes use cupping between training sessions for tight areas. We schedule it away from hard workouts and combine it with mobility work.

Who Should Avoid Cupping

Skip it over broken skin, sunburn, rashes, varicose veins, recent injuries or areas of swelling. Tell us about pregnancy, blood thinners, bleeding disorders or skin conditions.

Aftercare

Stay warm, hydrate, avoid hot tubs, saunas and intense sun on the area for the rest of the day, and keep exercise light until the next day.

Related Services

A Brief History

Cupping appears in ancient medical traditions across China, Egypt, Greece and the Middle East, where it is known as hijama. Early cups were made from animal horn, bamboo or clay. Modern clinics use glass, plastic or silicone cups, which are easier to sterilize and control.

Glass, Plastic or Silicone

Glass with fire. A brief flame inside the cup creates suction. The flame never touches the skin. This method gives a warm, traditional feel.

Plastic with a pump. A hand pump allows precise control of suction, useful for sensitive patients.

Silicone. Flexible cups are squeezed and released. They work well for moving cupping and smaller areas.

What We Track

We note which areas were treated, suction level, time and how you felt afterward, along with changes in pain or mobility. This helps refine future sessions.

Cupping and Sleep

Many people report feeling relaxed and sleeping well after a session. Scheduling cupping in the late afternoon or evening may suit those who want to rest afterward.

Planning Around Events

Because marks can last a week or more, schedule cupping with weddings, beach days and photo shoots in mind. Flash cupping or lighter suction can reduce marking when timing matters.
